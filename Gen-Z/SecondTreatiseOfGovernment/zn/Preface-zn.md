# PREFACE

Reader, you've got the intro and the outro of a discourse on government here; what happened to the papers that
should've filled the gap in between—which were actually the bulk of the work—isn't even worth mentioning. I
hope what remains is enough to validate the reign of our great restorer, King William; to secure his legit title
via the consent of the people (which is the only way any government is actually lawful), a title he's established
more clearly than any prince in Christendom; and to prove to the world the vibe of the English people, whose
dedication to their Natural Rights (the inherent specs) and their resolve to protect them saved the nation when
it was literally on the verge of systemic capture and ruin.

If these papers provide that evidence—and I'm feeling pretty good that they do—then there's no big loss for
the missing parts, and you'll be satisfied without them. I’ve neither the time nor the inclination to do the work
all over again and fill the gaps by re-tracing Sir Robert's logic through the absolute maze and smoke-and-mirrors
of his "wonderful" system.

The king and the whole nation have since entirely debunked his hypothesis, so I doubt anyone hereafter will have
the confidence to vibe-check our common safety and act as a shill for slavery, or be weak enough to be fooled
by contradictions packaged in a trendy aesthetic and eloquent phrasing. If anyone actually puts in the effort
to strip Sir Robert’s discourses of their vague flourishes and tries to reduce his words to direct, positive,
intelligible propositions and then compares them, they'll quickly realize it was just pure glib nonsense wrapped
in high-key eloquent English.

If you don't think it's worth checking his whole body of work, just try him on the topic of usurpation; see if
you can, with all your skill, make Sir Robert actually make sense or align with basic logic. I wouldn't speak so
plainly of a man who's long gone if the pulpit hadn't recently rebranded his doctrine as the mainstream dogma of
the era. It's necessary that those who claim to be teachers, but have dangerously gaslit the public, be openly
shown the shaky authority of the "Patriarch" they've been blindly stanning. They need to either take back the L's
they've been posting on such weak grounds, or justify the principles they preached as gospel, even though their
only source was an English courtier.

I wouldn't have written against Sir Robert or exposed his mistakes, inconsistencies, and his total lack of
scripture-proofs (which he loves to brag about) if there weren't people among us who, by hyping up his books,
save me from the reproach of fighting a dead opponent. They've been so zealous about this that if I've done him
any wrong, I can't expect them to be chill about it. I just wish that where they've done the truth and the public
a wrong, they'd be just as ready to fix it and acknowledge this: that nothing is more toxic for the prince and the
people than spreading mid-tier or flat-out wrong notions about governance—otherwise, every generation will just
keep complaining about the "Ecclesiastic Drum."

If anyone actually cares about the truth and wants to debunk my hypothesis, I promise to either recant my mistake
upon fair conviction or answer their difficulties. But they need to remember two things:

First, nitpicking a few phrases or small incidents in my discourse isn't a real response to my thesis.

Secondly, I don't take insults for actual arguments, nor do I find them worth my attention—though I'm always
down to give satisfaction to anyone who is genuinely scrupulous and can show a valid reason for their doubts.

One last thing: "Observations" refers to my observations on Hobbs, Milton, etc., and any page numbers refer to
his *Patriarcha*, 1680 Edition.
