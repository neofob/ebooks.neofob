# CHAPTER II

## OF THE STATE OF NATURE (THE OG STATE)

**Sect. 4.** To actually understand political power and where it comes from, we have to look at the default settings
for all humans: the State of Nature (the OG state). This is a state of peak freedom where people can run their own
lives and handle their assets and persons exactly how they see fit, within the boundaries of the law of nature,
without having to ask for permission or vibe-check their choices with any other person.

It's also a state of total equality. All power and jurisdiction are reciprocal—nobody has a natural "main
character" status over anyone else. It's pretty obvious that creatures of the same species and rank, born with
the same natural advantages and the same cognitive toolkit, should be equal without any built-in hierarchy, unless
the Creator explicitly decided to put someone above others and gave them a clear, undeniable right to rule.

**Sect. 5.** The judicious Hooker sees this natural equality as so self-evident that it's the foundation for why
we should actually care about each other. He uses it to build the duties we owe one another and derives the core
principles of justice and charity. In his words:

> The same natural logic has led men to realize that it's just as much their duty to love others as they love
themselves. Since things that are equal must share the same measure, if I want to receive good things from others
just as much as anyone else does, how can I expect that to happen unless I'm also looking out for their identical
desires? Since we're made of the same stuff, nature works the same way for everyone. To have something offered to
me that goes against this desire would hurt as much as it would hurt them; so if I do harm, I should expect to
suffer it back, because there's no reason others should love me more than I love them. My desire to be loved by
my equals in nature as much as possible creates a natural duty to give them that same energy. From this equality,
natural reason has drawn the rules for how to live—a vibe no rational person is ignorant of.

**Sect. 6.** Now, while this is a state of liberty, it's not a state of total chaos or "do whatever you want" energy
(it's not a state of licence). Even though a person in the State of Nature (the OG state) has uncontroulable freedom
over their own person or assets, they don't have the right to just destroy themselves or any creature they own,
unless a higher purpose than basic survival requires it.

The State of Nature (the OG state) has a law of nature governing it that everyone is bound by. Reason, which *is*
that law, teaches anyone who actually thinks about it that since we're all equal and independent, nobody should harm
another in their life, health, liberty, or assets. After all, we're all the work of one omnipotent and infinitely
wise Maker; we're all employees of one Sovereign Master, sent into the world on His mission. We are His property,
made to last for His pleasure, not each other's.

Since we have the same capabilities and share one common nature, there's no hierarchy that lets us destroy each
other, as if we were made for someone else's use like lower animals are for ours. Just as everyone is obligated
to preserve themselves and not just quit the game, for the same reason—when it doesn't conflict with their own
survival—they should do everything they can to preserve the rest of humanity. You can't just take away or damage
someone's life, liberty, health, or goods, unless it's to deliver justice to an offender.

**Sect. 7.** To stop everyone from invading each other's rights and to make sure the law of nature (which wants peace
and survival for all) is actually followed, the execution of the law in the State of Nature (the OG state) is handled
by everyone. This means everyone has the right to punish law-breakers to the extent that it stops the violation
from happening again. The law of nature would be totally useless—just like any other law in this world—if there
were nobody in the State of Nature (the OG state) with the power to execute it, protect the innocent, and restrain
the toxic. And if any one person can punish another for an evil act, everyone can. In a state of perfect equality,
where there's no natural boss, whatever one person can do to enforce the law, everyone else has that same right.

**Sect. 8.** And so, in the State of Nature (the OG state), one person can gain power over another. But it's
not some absolute or arbitrary power to just treat a criminal however they feel in a moment of heat or boundless
extravagance. It's only to give them a response, as calm reason and conscience dictate, that is proportionate to
their crime—enough for reparation and restraint. These are the only two valid reasons why one person can lawfully
harm another, which is what we call punishment.

By breaking the law of nature, the offender basically announces they've stopped using reason and common equity (the
measure God set for human actions to ensure mutual security). They become dangerous to everyone because they've
snapped the tie that keeps us safe from injury and violence. Since this is a crime against the whole human species
and the peace that the law of nature protects, any person—by their right to preserve humanity in general—can
restrain or, if necessary, eliminate things that are toxic to us. They can bring enough heat on an offender to
make them regret the act and stop others from doing the same. On these grounds, EVERY PERSON HAS THE RIGHT TO
PUNISH THE OFFENDER AND ACT AS THE EXECUTIONER OF THE LAW OF NATURE.

**Sect. 9.** I know this sounds like a wild take to some people. But before you cancel this doctrine, tell me:
by what right can a prince or a state execute or punish a foreigner for a crime they commit in that country? It's
obvious that their laws—even if they have the full backing of the legislature—don't apply to a stranger. Those
laws don't speak to them, and even if they did, the stranger isn't bound to listen. The legislative authority that
controls the citizens of a commonwealth has zero power over a foreigner. The people who make the laws in England,
France, or Holland are, to an Indian, just regular people without authority.

Therefore, if every person doesn't have the natural power to punish offences against the law of nature (as they
soberly judge the case to require), I don't see how the magistrates of any community can punish a foreigner;
because, in relation to that foreigner, they have no more power than what any random person naturally has over another.

**Sect. 10.** Beyond the crime of breaking the law and drifting from the right rule of reason (which makes a
person degenerate and a "toxic creature"), there's usually a specific person who gets hurt and takes damage from
the transgression. In that case, the person who was damaged has a specific right—on top of the general right to
punish shared by everyone—to seek reparation from the one who did it. Anyone else who thinks it's just can also
team up with the victim and help them recover whatever is needed to make things right.

**Sect. 11.** From these two distinct rights—the general right to punish for restraint and prevention (which
everyone has), and the right to take reparation (which only the victim has)—it happens that the magistrate (who
has the common right of punishing) can often waive the punishment for a crime if the public good doesn't require
it. But the magistrate *cannot* waive the satisfaction owed to a private person for the damage they took.

The person who suffered the damage has the right to demand it in their own name, and only they can cancel it. The
victim has the power to claim the goods or services of the offender as a right of self-preservation. Just as everyone
has the power to punish a crime to stop it from happening again (by their right to preserve humanity), every person
in the State of Nature (the OG state) has the power to kill a murderer. This does two things: it deters others from
doing the same (since no amount of money can fix a death) by showing that the whole community will come for them,
and it secures people from a criminal who, by rejecting reason, has declared war on all humanity. Such a person
can be taken out like a lion or a tiger—one of those wild animals you can't have any society or security with.

This is the basis for that great law of nature: *Whosoever sheddeth man’s blood, by man shall his blood be shed.*
Even Cain was so convinced that everyone had the right to destroy such a criminal that after he murdered his brother,
he cried out, "Every one that findeth me, shall slay me"; it was written that plainly in the hearts of all humans.

**Sect. 12.** For the same reason, a person in the State of Nature (the OG state) can punish smaller breaches
of the law. You might ask: "even with death?" My answer is that each crime should be punished to the degree and
severity that makes it a bad deal for the offender, makes them regret it, and scares others. Every offence that
can be committed in the State of Nature (the OG state) can be punished there just as it would be in a commonwealth.

While I'm not going into every detail of the law of nature and its specific punishment scales right now, it's
certain that such a law exists. It's just as clear to a rational person who studies it as the written laws of
states—maybe even clearer. Reason is easier to understand than the weird fantasies and complex schemes of men
who write laws to protect their own hidden interests. In fact, most national laws are only "right" to the extent
that they're based on the law of nature.

**Sect. 13.** To this "strange" doctrine—that everyone in the State of Nature (the OG state) has the executive
power of the law of nature—I'm sure people will object. They'll say it's unreasonable for people to be judges in
their own cases, because self-love will make them biased toward themselves and their friends. On the flip side,
they'll say that hate, passion, and revenge will make them go too far in punishing others, leading to total
chaos. They'll argue that God definitely appointed government to stop humans from being biased and violent.

I totally admit that civil government is the correct fix for the inconveniences of the State of Nature (the OG
state), which must be huge since people are judges in their own cases (it's easy to imagine that someone unjust
enough to hurt their brother won't be just enough to condemn themselves for it). But I want those making this
objection to remember that absolute monarchs are just men too. If government is supposed to be the fix for the
evil of people being judges in their own cases, and the State of Nature (the OG state) is therefore unbearable,
I want to know what kind of government is actually better than the OG state, where one man, commanding a crowd,
has the freedom to be the judge in his own case and can do whatever he wants to his subjects without anyone having
the right to question or control him? Whatever he does—whether by reason, mistake, or passion—must be submitted to.

It's way better to be in the State of Nature (the OG state), where you aren't forced to submit to the unjust will of
another. If the judge gets it wrong in their own case or any other, they are answerable for it to the rest of humanity.

**Sect. 14.** A common "big" objection is: where are these people in a State of Nature (the OG state), or have
they ever existed? To that, it's enough to say that since all princes and rulers of independent governments
worldwide are in a State of Nature (the OG state), it's clear the world has always had, and always will have,
numbers of people in that state. I'm talking about all governors of independent communities, whether they're in
a league with others or not. Not every agreement ends the State of Nature (the OG state) between men—only the
one where they agree to join one community and form one body politic.

Other promises and deals—like the ones made for trade between two men on a desert island (mentioned by Garcilasso
de la Vega) or between a Swiss person and an Indian in the American woods—are still binding, even though they
are perfectly in a State of Nature (the OG state) relative to each other. Truth and keeping one's word belongs to
humans as humans, not just as members of a formal society.

**Sect. 15.** To those who say there were never any humans in the State of Nature (the OG state), I'll point to
the authority of the judicious Hooker who says:

> The laws of nature bind men absolutely, just because they are men, even if they have no formal fellowship or
solemn agreement on what to do or not do. But since we aren't sufficient on our own to get all the things we need
for the kind of life our nature desires—a life fit for the dignity of man—we are naturally driven to seek
community and fellowship with others. This was the reason men first united into political societies.

But I also affirm that all humans are naturally in that state and stay there until they give their consent to
become members of some political society. I'll make this very clear as we move forward.
