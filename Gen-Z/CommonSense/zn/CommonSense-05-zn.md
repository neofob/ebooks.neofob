# AMERICA'S CURRENT VIBE CHECK & SOME RANDOM THOUGHTS

I've never met a single person, in England or the States, who hasn't admitted that we're gonna split
eventually. Honestly, we've been clueless trying to figure out if the Continent is "ready" or "ripe" for independence.

Everyone agrees it's happening; we just argue about *when*. To stop the cap, let's just look at the facts and find
the exact moment. Actually, we don't even need to look far—the moment is literally right now. Everyone's on the
same page; the vibes are aligned.

Our strength isn't in the numbers, it's in the unity. But even then, our numbers are enough to shut down the
rest of the world. Right now, the Continent has the biggest squad of trained soldiers under heaven. We've hit
that sweet spot where no single colony can carry itself, but together, we're an absolute unit. Any less or more,
and it would be a disaster. Our land game is already set. As for the navy, it's obvious Britain would never let
us build a warship while we're still under her thumb. Waiting a hundred years won't help—in fact, we'll be worse
off because we're running out of timber, and what's left will be too far away to get.

If the Continent was overcrowded, this whole situation would be unbearable. More port towns would just mean more
things to defend and more things to lose. Right now, our population is perfectly balanced for what we need—nobody
has to be idle. Trade is down, which gives us an army, and the army creates a new kind of trade.

We have zero debt. Anything we borrow for this cause will be a flex for our virtue. If we can leave the next
generation with a legit government and their own independent constitution, it's a bargain. But spending millions
just to get a few trash laws repealed and kick out the current administration? That's a scam. It's cruel to the
next generation because it leaves them with the actual hard work to do *and* a massive debt they didn't even
benefit from. That's some low-tier, small-minded politician energy.

Don't even stress about the debt we might take on if we actually get the job done. No nation should be debt-free. A
national debt is basically a national bond, and if there's no interest, it's not even a problem. Britain is drowning
in over 140 million pounds of debt and paying 4 million in interest. To make up for it, she has a huge navy. America
has no debt and no navy; for a fraction of Britain's debt, we could have a navy twice as big. England's navy isn't
even worth 3.5 million pounds right now.

No place on earth is better suited to build a fleet than America. We have the tar, timber, iron, and rope right
here. We don't need to import a thing. The Dutch make bank renting warships to Spain and Portugal, but they have
to import almost everything. We should treat building a fleet as a business—it's what this country is built
for. It's the smartest investment we can make. A finished navy is worth more than what it cost to build. It's
that perfect balance of making money and staying safe. Let's just build it; if we don't need them, we can sell
them and swap our paper money for actual gold and silver.

People are tripping when it comes to staffing a fleet; you don't need a quarter of the crew to be pro sailors. The
privateer Captain Death fought the hardest battle of the last war with fewer than twenty actual sailors on a crew
of over two hundred. A few skilled sailors can quickly teach a bunch of active land-lubbers how a ship works. We're
in the perfect position to start right now—the timber is there, the fisheries are blocked, and the shipwrights
are unemployed. We built 70 and 80-gun ships in New England forty years ago, so why not now? Ship-building is
America's biggest flex, and eventually, we'll be the best in the world. The big empires in the East are mostly
landlocked, so they can't compete. Africa is in shambles, and no European power has both a massive coastline and
the raw materials. Nature gave one or the other, but she gave America both. Russia is basically cut off from the
sea, so their forests and iron are just things they sell.

Safety-wise, should we be without a fleet? We aren't the small fry we were sixty years ago. Back then, we could've
left our stuff in the street and slept without locking the doors. Now, things are different, and our security needs
to scale with our wealth. A random pirate could've sailed up the Delaware a year ago and shaken down Philadelphia
for whatever he wanted. The same could happen anywhere. Any bold dude in a small brig could rob the whole Continent
and walk away with half a million. We need to pay attention—we need naval protection.

Some might say that once we make up with Britain, she'll protect us. Are we really that delusional? You want her
to keep a navy in our harbors? Common sense says the power that tried to crush us is the worst choice to protect
us. "Friendship" is just a cover for conquest; we'd fight a long, brave war only to be tricked into slavery. And
if we don't let her ships in our harbors, how is she supposed to protect us? A navy three or four thousand miles
away is useless in an emergency. If we have to protect ourselves, why not just do it? Why outsource it?

The English list of warships is long and scary, but less than a tenth of them actually work. Half aren't even built,
but they keep the names on the list for clout, even if there's only one plank left of the ship. And of the ones
that actually work, only a tiny fraction can be sent to one place at a time. Britain is stretched thin across
the East and West Indies, the Mediterranean, Africa, and everywhere else she claims to own. Because of bias and
ignorance, we've got a fake idea of the English navy; we talk like we'll have to fight the whole thing at once,
and we think we need a navy just as big. Since that's not possible right now, a few secret Tories use that to
scare us into not starting. That's a lie. If America had even a twentieth of Britain's naval power, we'd smoke
them. Why? Because we don't claim any foreign land. Our whole force would be right here on our own coast, giving
us a 2-to-1 advantage over people who have to sail thousands of miles to attack us and then sail all the way back
to refit. Britain might block our trade to Europe, but we have the same leverage over her trade to the West Indies,
which is right in our backyard and totally at our mercy.

We could even find a way to keep a navy during peacetime if we don't want a standing fleet. If we give bonuses to
merchants to build ships with 20 to 50 guns (bonuses based on how much cargo space they lose), 50 or 60 of those
ships plus a few guard ships would be enough. This avoids the problem England has where their fleet just sits in
the docks rotting during peace. Combining commerce and defense is a pro move; when our money and our muscle work
together, no external enemy can touch us.

We have everything we need for defense. Hemp is growing like crazy, so we've got rope. Our iron is better than anywhere
else. Our small arms are top-tier. We can cast cannons whenever we want. We're producing saltpeter and gunpowder
every day. We're getting smarter by the hour. Being determined is just who we are, and we've never lacked courage.

So what's the hold-up? Why are we hesitating? Britain is only bringing ruin. If she gets back into the government
of America, this place won't even be worth living in. There will be constant jealousy and riots, and who's going
to stop them? Who's going to risk their life to force their own people to obey a foreign power? The beef between
Pennsylvania and Connecticut over some land shows how useless the British government is, and proves that only a
Continental authority can handle Continental business.

Another reason why *now* is the time: since our population is still relatively small, there's more unoccupied
land. Instead of the King giving it away to his useless followers, we can use it to pay off the debt and support
the government. No nation on earth has an advantage like this.

The "infant state" of the Colonies—as they call it—isn't a weakness; it's a reason for independence. We're
numerous enough, and if we were bigger, we might be less united. It's interesting that the more crowded a country
is, the smaller its armies are. The ancients had way bigger armies than we do now, and it's obvious why: once
trade takes over because of population growth, people get too absorbed in making money to care about anything
else. Commerce kills the spirit of patriotism and defense. History shows that the greatest achievements always
happen when a nation is young. England lost its spirit as it got richer. London, despite its size, just takes
insults with the patience of a coward. The more people have to lose, the less they're willing to risk. Rich people
are basically slaves to fear and submit to power with the trembling fake-ness of a lapdog.

Youth is when you build good habits, for people and nations. It might be impossible to form the Continent into
one government fifty years from now. The different interests from trade and population growth would create total
chaos. Colony would be fighting colony. The strong would look down on the weak, and while the proud and foolish
bragged about their tiny differences, the wise would wish we'd united sooner. Now is the time. The bonds formed in
childhood and the friendships made during hardship are the most permanent. Our union has both: we're young and we've
been through it. Our agreement has survived the struggle, creating a legacy for the next generation to be proud of.

Now is also a unique moment that only happens once for a nation: the moment of creating its own government. Most
nations blow this chance and end up with laws forced on them by conquerors. They usually get a king first, then
a government. But the charter—the rules of the game—should come first, and then people are picked to run
it. Let's learn from other nations' mistakes and start the government from the right end.

When William the Conqueror took England, he gave them laws at sword-point. Until we legally and officially set up
the government in America, we're at risk of some lucky thug taking over and treating us the same way. Then where's
our freedom? Where's our property?

On religion: I believe it's the absolute duty of any government to protect everyone's right to believe what they
want, and that's the *only* thing the government should do regarding religion. Drop the narrow-mindedness and
selfishness that people in every profession cling to, and you'll stop being afraid. Suspicion is for small souls and
ruins society. Personally, I believe it's God's will that we have different religious opinions; it gives us more
chances to show Christian kindness. If we all thought the same, there'd be nothing to prove. On this principle,
I see the different denominations as children of the same family, just with different first names.

On page forty, I mentioned a Continental Charter (just hints, not a full plan). I'll bring it up again: a charter
is a solemn agreement that everyone signs to protect the rights of every part—whether it's religion, freedom,
or property. A fair deal and honest accounting make for long-term friends.

I also mentioned the need for large and equal representation, which is super important. A small number of voters
or representatives is dangerous. But if the number is both small *and* unequal, it's even worse. For example,
when the Associators' petition was before the Pennsylvania House of Assembly, only 28 members were there. The 8
from Bucks County voted against it; if 7 from Chester had done the same, the whole province would've been run by
just two counties. That's the risk. The way that House tried to grab power over the province's delegates should
warn everyone about trusting power out of their own hands. A set of instructions for the Delegates was put together
that was so brain-dead it would've embarrassed a schoolboy, and after a few people inside and outside approved it,
it was passed for the whole colony. If the whole colony knew how much that House hated necessary public measures,
they wouldn't trust them for a second.

Emergency needs can make things seem convenient that would become oppressive if they stayed. "Fast" and "Right"
are not the same thing. When America was in crisis, appointing people from the various Houses of Assembly was the
fastest and best way to handle it, and that wisdom saved the continent from ruin. But since we'll probably always
have a Congress, we need to think about how we choose its members. I'll ask the experts on humanity: is it too
much power for one body to handle both representation and election? When we plan for the future, remember that
virtue isn't hereditary.

We often learn great lessons from our enemies, or get pushed toward the truth by their mistakes. Mr. Cornwall
(one of the Lords of the Treasury) treated the New York Assembly's petition with contempt because it only had 26
members—he argued that such a "trifling" number couldn't possibly represent everyone. We thank him for being
accidentally honest. ¹

¹ If you want to understand why large and equal representation is a big deal, read Burgh's political disquisitions.

To wrap up: however strange it seems, or however much some people hate the idea, there are plenty of strong reasons
why nothing will fix our situation as fast as an open and determined declaration of independence. A few of them are:

First—usually, when two nations are at war, other powers step in as mediators to start peace talks. But as long
as America calls itself a subject of Great Britain, no power can offer to mediate. In our current state, we could
be fighting forever.

Secondly—it's unrealistic to think France or Spain will help us if we're just using that help to fix things with
Britain and make the connection stronger; those powers would lose out in the end.

Thirdly—as long as we claim to be British subjects, foreign nations see us as rebels. It's a dangerous precedent
for their own peace—people in arms while claiming to be subjects. We get the paradox, but combining resistance
and subjection is a concept way too complex for most people to wrap their heads around.

Fourthly—if we published a manifesto and sent it to foreign courts, explaining the misery we've endured and the
peaceful ways we tried to fix it—declaring that we can no longer live safely under the cruel British court and
are forced to break all ties—while assuring them we're peaceful and want to trade with them... that would do
more for the Continent than a whole ship full of petitions to Britain.

Under our current label as "British subjects," we aren't heard abroad. Every court's customs are against us,
and will be until we become an independent nation and take our place among equals.

This might seem weird and hard at first, but like everything else we've done, it'll feel normal soon. Until
independence is declared, the Continent will feel like a person who keeps putting off a gross chore every day,
knowing it has to be done, hating to start, wanting it over, and being haunted by the thought of it.
