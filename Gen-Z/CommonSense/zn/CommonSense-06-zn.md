# APPENDIX.

Since the first edition of this pamphlet dropped—actually, on the exact same day—the King's Speech hit the
city. If there was any prophecy involved in the birth of this work, it couldn't have come at a more perfect or
necessary time. The bloodlust in his words just proves why we need to double down on independence. People read
his speech and, instead of being scared, they felt the spark of a real, manly drive to split.

When we stay silent or keep things "formal" just to be polite, we're basically giving a pass to pure evil. If we
agree on that, then it's obvious: the King's Speech is a masterpiece of toxicity and deserves to be collectively
cancelled by the Congress and the people. But since a nation's peace depends on keeping its vibes clean, sometimes
it's better to treat some things with silent disdain rather than inventing new ways to hate that might mess with
our own stability. Maybe that's why the King's Speech hasn't been "publicly executed" yet. But let's be real:
this "speech" is nothing but a bold, audacious lie against the truth, the common good, and the very existence
of humanity. It's just a fancy way of sacrificing human lives to feed the ego of a tyrant. This kind of massacre
is just a "perk" of having a king. Nature doesn't know them, and they definitely don't know nature. Even though
we're the ones who created them, they've forgotten us and now think they're the gods of their own creators. The
only good thing about the speech is that it's not trying to trick anyone; we could never be deceived by it. The
brutality and tyranny are written all over it. Every line proves that the guy who hunts "savages" in the woods is
actually more of a savage than the King of Britain.

Then you've got Sir John Dalrymple, the "father" of that whining, fake piece called "The Address of the people
of England to the inhabitants of America." He probably thought we'd be intimidated by the pomp and circumstance
of a king, so he accidentally revealed the King's true character. He basically said, "If you're down to thank the
administration that repealed the Stamp Act, it's unfair not to thank the Prince who allowed them to do it with a
single nod." This is bootlicking in its purest form. This is idol worship without a mask. Anyone who can listen
to that and think it's okay has lost their grip on reality—they've betrayed the very idea of being a man. They
haven't just given up their dignity; they've sunk below the level of animals, crawling through the world like a worm.

At this point, it doesn't even matter what the King of England says or does. He's broken every moral and human
bond, trampled on nature and conscience, and through a steady stream of arrogance and cruelty, he's made the whole
world hate him. It's time for America to take care of her own. We have a big, young family to look after; it's our
duty to protect them rather than handing over our wealth to support a power that's a disgrace to humanity and to
faith. You—the moral leaders of this nation, regardless of your sect—and you, the guardians of our liberty:
if you want to keep this country clean from European corruption, you have to secretly wish for a split. But I'll
leave the moral side to your own reflections and focus on a few main points.

First: It is in America's best interest to leave Britain.
Second: Which is the easier, more realistic move—making up or breaking up?

On the first point, I could bring out the opinions of some of the smartest and most experienced people on this
continent (whose views aren't public yet), but it's actually a self-evident fact. No nation that's dependent on
another, limited in its trade, and chained in its laws can ever reach its full potential. America doesn't even know
what real wealth is yet. Our progress is insane compared to other nations, but it's still just the "childhood" phase
of what we could become if we actually had the power to make our own laws. England is greedily chasing something
that wouldn't even help her if she got it, while the Continent is hesitating on a choice that will be its final
ruin if we ignore it. England benefits from American trade, not conquest. That trade would mostly continue even
if we were as independent as France and Spain are from each other, because neither can find a better market. But
independence from Britain—or anyone else—is now the only goal worth fighting for. It's a truth that will only
get clearer every day.

Why?
First, because it's going to happen eventually anyway.
Second, because the longer we wait, the harder it'll be to pull off.

I've spent a lot of time listening to people talk without thinking. The most common take I hear is: "If this
split happened forty or fifty years from now, we'd be more capable of shaking off the dependence." My answer? Our
current military skill comes from the experience of the last war. In fifty years, that knowledge will be dead. We
wouldn't have a single General or officer left; we'd be as clueless about war as the ancient Indians. That one
point proves that *now* is the best time. Here's the logic: at the end of the last war, we had experience but not
enough people. In fifty years, we'll have the people but no experience. The perfect time is right now—the sweet
spot where we still have the experience and we've finally grown the numbers.

Pardon the tangent, but let's get back to the main point:
If we just "patch things up" with Britain and let her stay the boss (which, honestly, is just giving up), we lose the
only way to pay off our debts. The value of the backcountry lands—which some provinces are being robbed of because
of the fake borders of Canada—is worth over 25 million in Pennsylvania currency, with yearly rents of 2 million.

We can pay off the debt by selling those lands without burdening anyone, and the rents will eventually cover the
cost of government. It doesn't matter how long it takes to pay back the debt, as long as the land sales go toward
it. The current Congress should be the trustees for this.

Now to the second point: Which is the easier, more realistic plan—reconciliation or independence?

If you follow nature, you can't be tricked. Independence is a single, simple line we draw for ourselves. Reconciliation
is a messy, complicated nightmare where a treacherous, moody court gets to interfere. The answer is obvious.

The current state of America is honestly terrifying for anyone who thinks about it. No law, no government, no power
except what's granted by "courtesy." We're held together by a massive shared vibe, but that can change, and every
secret enemy is trying to kill it. We have legislation without law, wisdom without a plan, a constitution without
a name—and the weirdest part is that we have total independence but are still arguing about dependence. This is
unprecedented. No one's property is secure in this loose system. The crowd is just drifting, following whatever
fancy or opinion pops up because there's no fixed goal. Nothing is "criminal" anymore because there's no law; no
one's committing "treason" because there's no sovereign. Everyone just does whatever they want. The Tories wouldn't
have dared to gather so aggressively if they knew their lives were forfeit under the laws of the state. We need
a clear line: English soldiers captured in battle are prisoners, but Americans caught in arms are traitors. One
loses his freedom; the other loses his head.

Despite our wisdom, there's a visible weakness in how we're handling things, which just encourages the drama. The
"Continental Belt" is buckled too loosely. If we don't tighten it now, we'll hit a point where neither making up
nor breaking up is possible. The king and his useless cronies are playing their old game of dividing the continent,
and there are plenty of printers out there spreading fake news. That fake, hypocritical letter in the New York
papers recently proves there are people here who lack both judgment and honesty.

It's easy to hide in a corner and talk about "making up." But do those people actually think about how hard
that task is, or how dangerous it could be if the continent splits over the issue? Do they consider everyone
else—not just their own situation? Do they put themselves in the shoes of the sufferer who lost everything, or
the soldier who gave up everything to defend his home? If their "moderation" only fits their own private lives,
they're dreaming—they're "reckoning without their Host."

"Just put us back to how things were in '63," some say. My answer: Britain can't even do that if she wanted
to, and she won't. But even if she did, how can you trust a corrupt, faithless court to keep its word? Another
parliament—or even the current one—could just repeal the deal later, claiming it was forced on them. Where's
our rescue then? You can't sue a nation in court; cannons are the lawyers for crowns, and the sword of war decides
the case. To be "back in '63," it's not enough to just change the laws; we'd need our towns rebuilt, our private
losses paid back, and our public debts cleared. Otherwise, we're millions of dollars worse off than we were back
then. If this had been done a year ago, it would've won the heart of the continent—but now it's too late. The
Rubicon is passed.

Besides, taking up arms just to get a tax law repealed seems as wrong as taking up arms to force people to pay
it. The goal doesn't justify the means; human lives are too valuable to waste on trifles. What justifies the use
of arms is the violence done to us: the destruction of our property by an armed force, the invasion of our land
with fire and sword. The moment that happened, all loyalty to Britain ended. Independence started the second the
first musket was fired. This is a line of consistency—not based on a whim or ambition, but a chain of events we
didn't start.

I'll end with a few final hints. There are three ways independence can happen: by the legal voice of the people
in Congress, by a military power, or by a mob. One of these will be our fate. It might not always be the case
that our soldiers are citizens or that the crowd is reasonable; virtue isn't hereditary or permanent. If we do it
through the first way, we have the chance to build the purest, most noble constitution on earth. We can literally
start the world over again. Nothing like this has happened since the days of Noah. The birthday of a new world is
here, and millions of people are about to get their freedom based on what happens in a few months. It's an awesome
thought—and it makes the petty bickering of a few weak or selfish people look ridiculous compared to the fate
of a world.

If we waste this window and independence happens some other way, it's on us—or rather, on the narrow-minded
people who keep opposing it without even thinking. There are reasons for independence that should be pondered in
private rather than shouted in public. We shouldn't be debating *if* we'll be independent, but *how* to do it on a
firm, secure, and honorable basis. We should be stressed that we haven't even started yet. Every day proves it's
necessary. Even the Tories (if there are any left) should be the most eager to push for it; just as committees
protected them from the crowd at first, a stable government is the only way they'll actually stay safe. If they
aren't virtuous enough to be Whigs, they should at least be smart enough to want independence.

In short, independence is the only bond that can keep us together. Then we'll have a clear goal, and we can ignore
the schemes of a cruel enemy. We'll also be in a better position to negotiate with Britain; the crown's pride will
be less hurt by treating with "American states" for peace than with "rebellious subjects" for a favor. Our delay
just makes her think she can still win and drags out the war. We tried boycotting trade to get our grievances fixed,
and it didn't work. Now let's try the alternative: fix them ourselves as an independent nation, then offer to open
trade. The reasonable business people in England will be with us, because peace and trade are better than war and
nothing. And if they don't accept, other courts will.

That's where I leave it. Since no one has successfully refuted the points in the previous editions of this pamphlet,
it's a sign that either the logic is bulletproof, or the people who agree with it are too many to fight. So, instead
of looking at each other with suspicion, let's shake hands and draw a line that buries all past drama. Let the names
"Whig" and "Tory" die. Let the only names be "good citizen," "loyal friend," and "virtuous supporter of the rights
of mankind and the FREE AND INDEPENDENT STATES OF AMERICA."

To the Representatives of the Quakers (or whoever decided to publish that piece "The Ancient Testimony..."):

I'm one of the few who never mocks or picks apart religion. We're all accountable to God, not man. So, I'm not
addressing you as a religious group, but as a political body, messing with things that your own principles of
"quietude" tell you to avoid.

Since you've decided to speak for all Quakers without any real authority, I'll put myself in the place of everyone
who agrees with my principles. That way, you can see in me the same "presumption" you have in yourselves. Neither
of us has any right to represent anyone politically.

When people wander off the right path, they stumble. It's clear from how you handled your "testimony" that politics
isn't your lane. You've just mixed good and bad things together in a way that's unnatural and unfair.

I'll give you credit for the first two pages—the desire for peace is a natural, religious wish for everyone. On
that ground, we who are building an independent constitution are the most hopeful of all. Our goal is permanent
peace. We're tired of the beef with Britain, and the only way out is a final split. We're dealing with the pain
of today to ensure an endless peace tomorrow. We are steadily working to dissolve a connection that has already
filled our land with blood and will only cause more misery.

We aren't fighting for revenge or power; we aren't trying to conquer the world or rob others. We were attacked in
our own homes, under our own vines, on our own land. We see our enemies as highwaymen and home-invaders. Since the
civil law can't protect us, we're forced to use the military one—the sword—in the exact same way you've used
the rope in the past. Maybe we feel the pain of the victims across the continent more than you do. Don't mistake
"coldness of soul" for religion, or put a bigot in the place of a Christian.

O you partial ministers of your own principles! If bearing arms is a sin, then starting a war must be even worse. If
you're actually preaching from the heart and not just using religion as a political tool, prove it. Proclaim
your doctrine to our enemies. Send your "peace" messages to St. James's, to the commanders in Boston, to the
admirals robbing our coasts, and to the murderers in power. If you had the soul of Barclay, you'd tell the King to
repent. You'd warn the Royal Wretch of his sins. You wouldn't just attack the victims; you'd call out everyone. Don't
claim you're being persecuted—you're bringing that on yourselves because you're pretending to be Quakers while
ignoring the core of the faith.

(Barclay's address to Charles II: "You've tasted prosperity and adversity... you know how hateful the oppressor
is to God and man... if you don't turn to the Lord... great will be your condemnation. Apply yourself to the light
of Christ in your conscience, which won't flatter you or let you be at ease in your sins.")

It seems from some of your actions that you think the only sin is the people bearing arms. You've mistaken political
loyalty for conscience. Your actions are inconsistent. It's hard to believe your "scruples" when the same men who
shout against the "mammon of this world" are chasing it with a hunger as sharp as Death.

The quote you used from Proverbs—that when a man's ways please the Lord, his enemies will be at peace with him—was
a terrible choice. It actually proves that the King's ways *don't* please the Lord, because he's definitely not
at peace.

Now to the last part of your testimony:
"It has always been our judgment... that the setting up and putting down of kings and governments is God's peculiar
prerogative... and that it is not our business to have any hand in it... but to pray for the king..."

If these are your principles, why aren't you following them? Why aren't you leaving "God's Work" to God? Your
own rules tell you to wait with patience and accept the outcome as divine will. Why the political testimony
then? Publishing it proves you either don't believe it or aren't brave enough to live it.

Quaker principles should make a man a quiet subject of any government. And if God is the one who handles kings, then
He clearly approves of everything that happens to them. Oliver Cromwell thanks you. Charles I didn't die by human
hands; and if the current "Proud Imitator" meets the same end, you're bound by your own doctrine to cheer. Kings
aren't removed by miracles; they're removed by human means—like the ones we're using now. Even the dispersion
of the Jews was done with arms. Since you refuse to help one side, you shouldn't mess with the other. Unless you
can prove that God—who put this new world as far as possible from the old one—actually wants us tied to the
corrupt court of Britain, you have no right to tell the people to "abhor" the idea of independence. What a joke! The
same people who just said the fate of kings is in God's hands are now trying to run the business themselves. The
inconsistency is glaring; the absurdity is laughable. This wasn't written by the whole body of Quakers, but by a
small, desperate political faction.

That's the end of the critique. I don't ask you to "abhor" it, just read it and judge it fairly. "Setting up and
putting down kings" means making a king out of someone who isn't one, or removing one who is. Neither applies
here. We aren't making or unmaking anything; we're just opting out. Your testimony only shames your judgment and
should've stayed in the drawer.

Why?
First, because mixing religion with political disputes brings reproach to all religion and is dangerous for society.
Second, because many Quakers don't even agree with this political testimony.
Third, because it threatens the continental harmony and friendship that your own charitable donations helped build.

I bid you farewell without anger. I sincerely wish that you enjoy every civil and religious right, and that you
help others secure theirs. But I hope the example of mixing religion with politics is rejected by everyone in America.
