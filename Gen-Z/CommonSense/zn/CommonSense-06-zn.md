# APPENDIX

Since the first edition of this pamphlet dropped—actually, on the same day—the King's Speech appeared in
the city. If this book were written by a psychic, they couldn't have timed it better. The King's bloodthirsty
energy just proves why we need to double down on independence. People read this as a reason for revenge. Instead
of scaring us, the Speech just paved the way for the boss-level principles of Independence.

When people stay silent or follow "protocol," it's toxic if it gives any cover to evil behavior. So, if we agree on
that, then the King's Speech—a masterpiece of villainy—deserves to be absolutely roasted by Congress and every
single person. But since a nation's peace depends on keeping things classy, sometimes it's better to just ignore
some things with total disdain rather than introducing new ways of hating that might mess up our stability. Maybe
that's why the King's Speech hasn't been "publicly executed" yet. The "Speech," if you can even call it that,
is just a bold-faced lie against the truth, the common good, and humanity itself. It's a fancy way of sacrificing
people to the ego of tyrants. But this kind of mass casualty event is just a perk of being a King; nature doesn't
know them, so they don't know nature. Even though we created them, they don't know us—they've become gods to the
people who made them. The one good thing about the Speech is that it doesn't even try to deceive us; we couldn't
be fooled by it if we wanted to. The brutality and tyranny are right there on the surface. It's crystal clear:
the guy who hunts "savages" in the woods is less of a savage than the King of Britain.

Sir John Dalrymple—the "father" of a whining, slippery piece of writing called "The Address of the people of
England to the inhabitants of America"—probably thought people here would be intimidated by the pomp of a king. He
actually (and stupidly) described the current King's real character. He says, "But if you're into paying compliments
to an administration we don't complain about" (talking about Rockingham's repeal of the Stamp Act), "it's unfair to
deny them to the prince who allowed them to do it with a single nod." This is peak Tory energy! It's straight-up
idol worship without a mask. Anyone who can calmly listen to and accept that logic has lost their mind—they've
basically quit being a man. They've not only given up human dignity but have sunk below the level of animals,
crawling through the world like a worm.

At this point, it doesn't even matter what the King of England says or does. He's broken every moral and human rule,
trampled on nature and conscience, and through constant arrogance and cruelty, earned universal hate. It's time
for America to take care of itself. We have a huge, young family to look after, which is more important than giving
away our money to support a power that's a joke to the names of men and Christians. You—whether you're religious
leaders of any sect or the guardians of public liberty—if you want to keep your country from being contaminated
by European corruption, you have to secretly want a separation. But leaving the moral stuff for private thought,
I'll stick to these points:

First: It's in America's best interest to split from Britain.

Secondly: Which is the easier, more doable plan: trying to make up or just going independent? (with some side notes).

To support the first point: if I thought it was a good idea, I could bring in opinions from some of the smartest,
most experienced people on this continent whose views aren't public yet. But honestly, it's self-evident. No nation
that's dependent on another, limited in trade, and tied up in its own laws can ever really make it big. America
doesn't even know what "rich" is yet. Even though our progress is unmatched in history, we're still in the "toddler"
phase compared to what we could be if we actually controlled our own laws. England is out here greedily wanting
things that wouldn't even help her; meanwhile, the Continent is hesitating on a decision that will totally destroy
us if we ignore it. England benefits from America's *trade*, not its *conquest*. That trade would mostly continue
even if we were as independent as France and Spain, because neither can find a better market. But independence
from Britain or anyone else is the only thing worth fighting for now, and like all truths found by necessity,
it'll become clearer every day.

1. Because it's going to happen eventually anyway.
2. Because the longer we wait, the harder it'll be to pull off.

I've often just sat back in public and private and watched people say things without thinking. The most common take
I hear is: "If this split happened 40 or 50 years from now instead of today, the Continent would be better equipped
to shake things off." My response: our military skill right now comes from the experience of the last war. In 40 or
50 years, that experience will be totally gone. We wouldn't have a single General or military officer left; we'd be
as clueless about war as the ancient Indians. This one point proves that *now* is the best time. The logic is this:
at the end of the last war, we had the skill but not the numbers. In 40 or 50 years, we'll have the numbers but
no skill. The perfect time is right in the middle—where we still have enough experience and have grown enough
in numbers. That time is right now.

Sorry for the tangent. Back to the main point:

If things get "patched up" with Britain and she stays in charge (which, given how things are, means we're giving
up entirely), we'll lose the only way to pay off our debts. The value of the back lands—which some provinces are
being secretly robbed of by the unfair expansion of Canada's borders—is worth over 25 million in Pennsylvania
currency (even at a low price of 5 pounds per 100 acres), and the rent would be 2 million a year.

Selling those lands is how the debt gets paid without anyone suffering, and the rent will eventually cover the
government's yearly costs. It doesn't matter how long it takes to pay off the debt, as long as the land sales go
toward it. The current Congress will act as the trustees for the continent.

Now to the second point: Which is easier and more doable, reconciliation or independence?

If you follow nature, you can't be fooled. My answer is simple: independence is a straight line we
control. Reconciliation is a mess of complications where a treacherous, moody court gets to interfere. That's the
answer right there.

The current state of America is genuinely scary for anyone who thinks about it. No law, no government, nothing
but power granted by "courtesy." We're held together by an insane level of agreement, but that can change, and
every secret enemy is trying to break it. We have legislation without law, wisdom without a plan, a constitution
without a name, and—which is the wild part—perfect independence fighting for dependence. This has no precedent;
it's never happened before. Who knows how it ends? Nobody's property is safe in this loose system. The crowd is
just drifting, following whatever random opinion pops up because there's no fixed goal. Nothing is "criminal" and
there's no such thing as treason, so everyone thinks they can just do whatever they want. The Tories wouldn't have
dared to gather aggressively if they knew their lives were forfeit under state law. There should be a clear line:
English soldiers captured in battle are prisoners, but Americans caught in arms are traitors. One loses their
freedom; the other loses their head.

Despite our wisdom, some of our moves are visibly weak, which encourages people to fight. The "Continental Belt"
is buckled too loosely. If we don't fix it now, it'll be too late, and we'll end up in a spot where neither
reconciliation nor independence is possible. The King and his useless followers are playing their old game of
dividing the Continent, and we have printers out here spreading fake news. That fake, hypocritical letter in the
New York papers a few months ago proves there are people who are either clueless or dishonest.

It's easy to hide in corners and talk about "making up." But do these people actually think about how hard that
task is, or how dangerous it is if the Continent splits over it? Do they consider the different types of people
involved? Do they put themselves in the shoes of the person who's already lost everything, or the soldier who gave
up everything to defend his country? If their "moderation" only fits their own private lives and ignores everyone
else, they're totally miscalculating.

Some say, "Just put us back to how we were in '63." My answer: Britain can't even do that, and she won't. But
even if she did, how do you trust a corrupt, faithless court to keep its word? Another parliament—or even this
one—could just cancel the deal later, claiming it was forced on them. Then what? You can't sue a nation. Cannons
are the lawyers for Kings; the sword of war, not justice, decides the case. Just putting the laws back to '63 isn't
enough; our *situation* has to be the same. Our burned towns need to be rebuilt, our private losses paid back, and
our public debts cleared. Otherwise, we're millions worse off than we were then. If this had happened a year ago,
it would've won everyone over—but now it's too late. The Rubicon has been crossed.

Also, taking up arms just to force the repeal of a tax law seems as wrong by divine law and as weird to human
feelings as taking up arms to force people to follow that law. The goal doesn't justify the means; human lives
are too valuable to throw away over trifles. It's the violence done to us—the destruction of our property by
an army and the invasion of our country with fire and sword—that justifies using arms. The second that became
necessary, all loyalty to Britain should have ended. America's independence should be dated from the first musket
fired against her. This is a consistent line; it's not based on a whim or ambition, but on a chain of events the
colonies didn't start.

I'll end these notes with some timely hints. There are three ways independence can happen, and one of them will
eventually be America's fate: by the legal voice of the people in Congress, by military power, or by a mob. It might
not always be that our soldiers are citizens and the crowd is reasonable; virtue isn't hereditary or permanent. If
it happens through the first way, we have every chance to create the noblest, purest constitution on earth. We
can literally start the world over. Something like this hasn't happened since Noah. The birth of a new world is
here, and millions of people are about to get their freedom based on what happens in a few months. It's an awesome
thought—and from that perspective, the petty bickering of a few weak or selfish people looks ridiculous compared
to the business of a whole world.

If we blow this window and independence happens some other way, it's on us—or rather, on the narrow-minded
people who constantly oppose it without thinking. There are reasons for independence that people should think about
privately rather than be told publicly. We shouldn't be debating *if* we'll be independent, but how to do it on a
solid, secure, and honorable basis. We should be stressed that we haven't even started yet. Every day proves it's
necessary. Even the Tories (if any are left) should be the ones pushing for it; just as committees protected them
from the mob at first, a legit government is the only way they'll stay safe. If they aren't virtuous enough to be
Whigs, they should at least be smart enough to want independence.

Basically, independence is the only bond that can keep us together. Then we'll see our goal, and we can legally
ignore the schemes of a cruel enemy. We'll be in a better position to negotiate with Britain; the court's pride
will be less hurt by dealing with "American states" for peace than with "rebellious subjects" for a compromise. Our
delay just makes her hope she can conquer us, and our hesitation just drags out the war. Since boycotting trade
didn't work to fix our grievances, let's try the other way: fix them ourselves independently, and *then* offer
to open trade. The reasonable business people in England will still be on our side, because peace with trade is
better than war without it. And if they don't take the offer, we can go to other courts.

That's where I'll leave it. Since no one has managed to disprove the points in the earlier editions of this pamphlet,
it's a sign that either the logic is bulletproof or the people who agree with it are too many to fight. So instead
of looking at each other with suspicion, let's shake hands and unite. Let's draw a line that buries every old
fight. Let the labels "Whig" and "Tory" die. Let the only labels be "good citizen," "loyal friend," and "virtuous
supporter of human rights and the FREE AND INDEPENDENT STATES OF AMERICA."

To the Representatives of the Religious Society of the People called Quakers (or whoever was involved in publishing
that piece about the "Ancient Testimony and Principles of the People called Quakers" regarding the King and
Government):

I'm one of the few people who never disrespects religion by making fun of any denomination. We're all accountable
to God, not man, for our faith. So, I'm not addressing you as a religious group, but as a political body messing
with things that your own principles of "Quietude" tell you to ignore.

Since you've put yourselves in the position of speaking for all Quakers without actual authority, I'll do the same
and speak for everyone who agrees with the principles you're attacking. I'm doing this so you can see the same
"presumption" in me that you can't see in yourselves. Neither of us has any right to "Political Representation."

When people wander off the right path, it's no wonder they trip. It's obvious from how you handled your testimony
that politics isn't your lane as a religious body. Even if it seems a good idea to you, it's just a jumble of good
and bad put together poorly, and your conclusion is unnatural and unfair.

We give you credit for the first two pages (the whole thing isn't even four pages long) and expect the same
civility back, because the desire for peace isn't just a Quaker thing—it's a natural human and religious wish
for everyone. On that basis, as people working to establish our own independent constitution, we are more hopeful
and driven than anyone. Our goal is permanent peace. We're tired of fighting Britain and see no end but a final
split. We're acting consistently; for the sake of lasting peace, we're dealing with the pain of today. We're
steadily working to dissolve a connection that has already filled our land with blood and will only cause more
trouble for both countries if it persists.

We aren't fighting for revenge or conquest, pride or passion. We aren't flexing our fleets and armies on the world
or looting the globe. We're being attacked in our own gardens, in our own homes, and on our own land. We see our
enemies as highwaymen and burglars. Since the civil law can't protect us, we have to use military law and the
sword—the same way you've used the noose before. Maybe we feel for the ruined sufferers across the continent
with a tenderness that hasn't reached your hearts yet. But don't confuse your cause. Don't call a "cold soul"
religion, and don't put a bigot in the place of a Christian.

You partial ministers of your own principles: if bearing arms is a sin, then starting a war must be even
worse—there's a huge difference between a deliberate attack and an unavoidable defense. If you're really
preaching from the heart and not just using religion as a political prop, prove it by telling your doctrine to
our enemies—because they're bearing arms too. Prove you're sincere by publishing your message at St. James's,
to the commanders in Boston, to the Admirals and Captains pirating our coasts, and to all the murdering thugs
acting under the guy you claim to serve. If you had the honest soul of Barclay ¹, you'd preach repentance to your
king. You'd tell that Royal Wretch about his sins and warn him of eternal ruin. You wouldn't just attack the injured
and insulted; you'd be faithful ministers and call out everyone. Don't say you're being persecuted, and don't try to
make us the villains; we're not complaining because you're Quakers, but because you pretend to be Quakers and aren't.

¹ "You've tasted both success and failure; you know what it's like to be kicked out of your country, to be bossed
around as well as to boss others, and to sit on a throne; and since you've been oppressed, you know how much God
and man hate the oppressor. If after all this, you don't turn to the Lord with all your heart but follow lust and
vanity, you're going to be in big trouble. The best remedy is to follow the light of Christ in your conscience,
which won't flatter you or let you be okay with your sins."
-- Barclay's address to Charles II.

Sadly, it seems like some of your testimony and conduct suggest that the only sin is bearing arms—and only when
the "people" do it. You've mistaken party loyalty for conscience because your actions aren't consistent. It's hard
to believe your "scruples" when we see the same people shouting about the "mammon of this world" while chasing
after it with a steady step and a huge appetite.

The quote you used from Proverbs—"when a man's ways please the Lord, he maketh even his enemies to be at peace
with him"—was a terrible choice. It actually proves that the King's ways (the guy you want to support) *don't*
please the Lord, otherwise his reign would be peaceful.

Now to the last part of your testimony, which everything else was just an intro for:

"It has always been our judgment and principle... that the setting up and putting down of kings and governments
is God's peculiar prerogative... and that it is not our business to have any hand in it... but to pray for the
king, and safety of our nation... that we may live a peaceable and quiet life... under the government which God
is pleased to set over us."

If these are really your principles, why aren't you following them? Why aren't you leaving "God's Work" to God? These
very principles tell you to wait patiently and humbly for the outcome of public events and accept it as divine
will. So why the political testimony if you actually believe this? Publishing it proves you either don't believe
it or aren't brave enough to practice it.

Quaker principles basically make a person a quiet, harmless subject of any government. And if changing governments
is God's job, He won't be robbed of it by us; so that principle means you should approve of *everything* that
happens to kings as His work. Oliver Cromwell thanks you. Charles didn't die by human hands, then. And if the
current "Proud Imitator" of Charles meets the same end, you're bound by your own doctrine to cheer. Kings aren't
removed by miracles; government changes happen through common human means—the same ones we're using now. Even
the dispersion of the Jews, though prophesied, was done with arms. Since you refuse to be the means on one side,
you shouldn't be meddlers on the other; just wait and see. Unless you have divine proof that the Almighty—who
put this new world as far as possible from the old one—disapproves of it being independent from the corrupt
British court... well, how can you justify stirring people up to "unite in the abhorrence" of writings that want
to break the "happy connection" and "necessary subordination" to the king? What a joke! The people who just said
that changing governments is God's business are now jumping in to take a share of the work. The inconsistency is
blinding; the absurdity is laughable. This could only be written by someone with a narrow, crabby spirit from a
desperate political party. You aren't the "whole body of Quakers," just a small, biased fraction.

That's the end of the critique. I'm not asking everyone to "abhor" it like you did, just to read it and judge
fairly. One last thing: "setting up and putting down kings" means making someone a king who isn't one, or making a
king not a king. What does that have to do with us? We don't want to make or unmake anyone; we just want *nothing*
to do with them. Your testimony just makes you look bad and should've stayed unpublished.

First, because it makes religion look bad and is dangerous for society to make faith a party in political fights.

Secondly, because it claims to speak for a group of men, many of whom hate the idea of publishing political
testimonies.

Thirdly, because it threatens the continental harmony and friendship that your own charitable donations helped
build—and keeping that is super important for all of us.

I bid you farewell without anger. I sincerely hope that as humans and Christians, you always enjoy every civil
and religious right, and help others get them too. But I hope the example you've set—mixing religion with
politics—is rejected by everyone in America.
