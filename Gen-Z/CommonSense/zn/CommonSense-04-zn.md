# VIBES ON THE CURRENT STATE OF AMERICAN AFFAIRS.

In the following pages, I'm just dropping straight facts, clear logic, and common sense. All I ask from you is to
drop the bias, clear your head, and let your own reason and feelings decide what's up. Be a real one—don't just
think about today, but look at the big picture for the future.

People have written entire books on the war between England and America. Everyone from every walk of life has
jumped into the drama for their own reasons, but talking hasn't worked. The debate is over. Now, it's coming down
to a fight. The King made the first move, and the continent just accepted the challenge.

There was this guy, Mr. Pelham, who was pretty good at his job but had some flaws. When people in the House of
Commons told him his plans were just temporary fixes, he basically said, "They'll last long enough for me." If
the colonies have that same "I don't care about the future" energy right now, future generations are going to
absolutely hate us for it.

The sun has never shined on a cause more important. This isn't just about one city, one state, or one kingdom—it's
about a whole continent, like one-eighth of the habitable world. This isn't a "right now" problem; it's a "forever"
problem. What we do now affects people until the end of time. We're planting the seeds for continental unity,
trust, and honor. Any crack we leave now is like carving a name into a baby oak tree; as the tree grows, the scar
gets huge, and the future will see it in giant letters.

By switching from talking to fighting, we've entered a new era of politics. The whole way of thinking has changed. Any
plan or proposal from before April 19th (when the fighting started) is basically like last year's calendar—it
might have been right then, but it's useless now. Back then, both sides actually wanted a union with Great Britain;
they just disagreed on *how* to do it—one side wanted force, the other wanted friendship. But the force failed,
and the friendship vibe is gone.

Everyone's been talking about the "benefits of making up," but that's just a nice dream that's over. It's time
we looked at the other side: the actual damage these colonies take, and will always take, by being tied to and
dependent on Great Britain. Let's use logic and common sense to see what we'd actually have if we split, and what
we can expect if we stay.

Some people claim that because America did well while tied to Britain, we need that connection to stay happy. That
logic is a total fail. That's like saying because a baby thrived on milk, it should never eat solid food, or
that the first 20 years of your life set the rules for the next 20. Actually, it's even simpler: America would've
done just as well, probably better, if no European power had ever touched us. We get rich selling the basics of
life—and as long as Europe likes to eat, we'll always have a market.

"But they protected us!" some say. Actually, they just locked us down. They defended the continent using our money
as well as theirs. They would've defended Turkey for the same reason: trade and power.

We've been blinded by old biases and superstitions. We bragged about Britain's "protection" without realizing
their motive was profit, not love. They didn't protect us from *our* enemies; they protected us from *their*
enemies. People who have no beef with us are only our enemies because we belong to Britain. If Britain stopped
claiming us, or if we dumped them, we'd be chill with France and Spain even if they were at war with Britain. The
mess in Hanover during the last war should be a warning: don't get tied up in these connections.

Parliament recently claimed that the colonies only know each other through the "parent country"—like Pennsylvania
and the Jerseys are "sisters" because of England. That's a very weird way to prove you're related, but it's a
great way to prove you're enemies. France and Spain aren't our enemies as Americans; they're our enemies because
we're subjects of Britain.

"But Britain is the parent country!" Again, that just makes their behavior even worse. Even animals don't eat
their own babies, and "savages" don't go to war against their own families. If that "parent" claim is true, it's
just a reason to roast them. In reality, the phrase "mother country" was just a sneaky tactic used by the King and
his yes-men to manipulate our emotions. Europe, not England, is the parent of America. This new world has been a
safe haven for people from all over Europe who were fleeing religious and political persecution. They didn't come
here to be hugged by a "mother"; they came here to escape a monster. And England is the worst of the bunch—the
same tyranny that drove the first people away is still chasing their grandkids.

In this huge part of the world, we can forget the tiny borders of England (which is only 360 miles long) and think
bigger. we're brothers with every European Christian. It's a much more based way to think.

It's funny how our biases shrink as we travel. A guy in a small English town only cares about his neighbors. If
he goes a few miles, he cares about his town. If he travels to another county, he cares about his county. But if
he goes to France, he suddenly feels like an "Englishman." By that same logic, all Europeans meeting in America
are just "countrymen." To a continental mind, the difference between England, Holland, Germany, or Sweden is as
small as the difference between a street and a town. Less than a third of the people here are even of English
descent. So calling England the "mother country" is just false, selfish, and narrow-minded.

Even if we *were* all English, so what? Nothing. Since Britain is now an open enemy, titles don't matter. Saying
we "owe" it to them to make up is a joke. The first king of the current line (William the Conqueror) was French,
and half the English Peers come from France too. By that logic, France should be ruling England.

People talk about how Britain and the colonies together could "defy the world." That's just a guess; war is
unpredictable. Plus, this continent isn't going to let its people be drained away just to fight British wars in
Asia, Africa, or Europe.

And why would we want to defy the world? Our goal is trade. If we focus on that, we'll have the friendship of all
Europe because it's in everyone's interest for America to be a free port. Our trade is our protection, and the
fact that we don't have tons of gold and silver means invaders won't care about us.

I dare anyone who wants to make up with Britain to name *one single advantage* we get from this connection. I'll
say it again: zero. Our corn will sell in any European market, and we can buy our imports from whoever we want.

But the damage we take from this connection is endless. Our duty to humanity and ourselves is to dump this
alliance. Any dependence on Britain just drags us into European wars and makes us enemies with nations that would
otherwise be our friends. Since Europe is our market, we shouldn't be tied to just one part of it. America's real
interest is to stay out of European drama, which we can't do as long as we're just a pawn in British politics.

Europe has too many kingdoms to ever be truly at peace. Every time England fights someone, American trade gets
wrecked. The next war might not be like the last; if it isn't, the people pushing for "reconciliation" now will
be the first to beg for separation then, because being neutral is a much safer bet than having a warship for a
bodyguard. Everything natural says we need to split. The blood of the dead is crying out: "It's time to part." Even
the distance between England and America is proof that they weren't meant to rule us. The timing of when America
was found also proves it—it's like the Universe opened a sanctuary for the persecuted just as things were getting
bad back home.

Britain's rule over this continent is a government that *has* to end. You can't find any real joy looking forward
when you know your "constitution" is just a temporary fix. As parents, we can't be happy knowing this government
isn't stable enough to leave anything behind for our kids. Since we're leaving the next generation in debt, we
should at least do the hard work of fixing the government now, otherwise we're just being cruel to them. We need
to imagine ourselves a few years in the future to see the reality that our current fears and biases are hiding.

I don't want to be mean, but I think everyone pushing for reconciliation fits into one of these groups:
1. People with a financial interest (can't be trusted).
2. People who are too blind to see the truth.
3. People who refuse to see the truth.
4. "Moderate" people who think Europe is better than it actually is.
This last group is actually the most dangerous because their "middle ground" approach will cause more disasters
than the other three combined.

Many people are lucky enough to live far from the tragedy, so they don't realize how precarious their property
actually is. But just imagine Boston for a second. That city is a nightmare. People who were rich and comfortable
a few months ago are now starving or begging. If they stay, their "friends" burn their houses; if they leave,
the soldiers rob them. They're prisoners with no way out, caught between two armies.

People with "chill" personalities tend to overlook Britain's crimes and say, "Come on, we'll be friends again." But
let's use a real-world vibe check: can you actually love, honor, and serve a power that brought fire and sword
into your land? If you can't, you're just lying to yourself and ruining the future. A connection based on
"convenience" will just collapse into something even worse. And if you say you can move past it, I have to ask:
Has your house been burned? Has your stuff been destroyed right in front of you? Are your wife and kids without a

if you *have* suffered and you're still shaking hands with the murderers, then you're not a real husband, father,
or friend—you're just a coward and a suck-up.

I'm not trying to hype people up or exaggerate; I'm just using the feelings that nature gives us. Without these
feelings, we couldn't function in society. I'm not showing you horror to make you vengeful, but to wake you up from
a lazy sleep so we can actually get things done. Britain and Europe can't conquer America unless America conquers
herself with hesitation. This winter is a golden opportunity; if we waste it, the whole continent suffers. Anyone
who wastes this time deserves the worst.

It's against all logic and the natural order of things to think this continent can stay subject to an outside
power. Even the people in Britain don't believe it. No amount of "wisdom" can find a plan other than separation
that gives us even one year of security. Reconciliation is a fake dream. Nature has already quit this relationship,
and "Art" can't fix it. As Milton said, you can't really make up when the wounds of hate are this deep.

Every peaceful attempt has failed. Our prayers were ignored; it just proved that nothing feeds a King's ego more
than being begged. That's how the kings of Europe became absolute—look at Denmark and Sweden. Since nothing
works but hitting back, for God's sake, let's just split. Don't leave the next generation to kill each other over
the fake titles of "parent" and "child."

Saying they'll never try to hurt us again is delusional. We thought that when the stamp-act was repealed, and we
were proven wrong in a year. Just because a nation was defeated once doesn't mean they won't try again.

As for governing, Britain can't do justice to this continent. The job is too big and too complex to be handled by
a power that's thousands of miles away and has no clue how we actually live. If they can't conquer us, they can't
govern us. Running 4,000 miles with a petition and waiting months for an answer that takes *another* few months
to explain is just childish. It might have worked once, but that time is over.

Small islands that can't protect themselves are fine to be part of a kingdom. But the idea of a whole continent
being ruled by a tiny island is absurd. In nature, the moon isn't bigger than the planet. England and America flip
that order, which proves they belong to different systems: England belongs to Europe, and America belongs to itself.

I'm not saying this because of pride, politics, or spite. I'm saying it because I'm 100% convinced that separation
and independence are the only way forward. Anything else is just a patch job that won't last. It's leaving the
sword for our children to deal with, instead of doing the work now to make this continent the glory of the earth.

Since Britain isn't even trying to compromise, we know we won't get any deal that's actually worth the blood and

The goal should match the cost. Getting rid of North or the other corrupt officials isn't worth millions of
dollars. A temporary trade stop was an inconvenience, but it would've been worth it if they'd repealed the acts. But

seeing how fast we've grown, it's coming soon. Starting the fight now wasn't about arguing—it was about being

But let's say we actually "made up." What then? The continent is ruined. For several reasons:

First, the King still has the final say on all our laws. He's already shown he's an enemy of liberty and craves

King wants us to be—do you think he'll let us grow? Bottom line: is a power that's jealous of our success the
right power to lead us? If you say "No," you're an independent. Independence just means: do we make our own laws,
or does the King—our biggest enemy—tell us what we can and can't do?

"But the King has a veto in England too!" True. But there's something ridiculous about a 21-year-old (which happens)

King lives in England, and he doesn't live in America. A veto here is ten times more dangerous. In England, he'll

America is just a side project in British politics. England only cares about us if it helps them. Their interest

But the strongest point is that only independence—a continental government—can keep the peace and stop civil

The colonies have shown they can handle their own government with order and obedience. Any fear that one colony

When everyone is equal, nobody can be "superior." Republics in Europe are generally at peace. Holland and Switzerland

If there's any real reason to fear independence, it's because we don't have a plan yet. People don't see the way
out. So, I'm offering some hints—not as the perfect solution, but as a starting point. If we collect the thoughts

Here's a rough idea:
Assemble annually with only a President. Make representation more equal. Focus on domestic issues and answer to
a Continental Congress.

Divide each colony into 6-10 districts. Each district sends delegates to Congress, so each colony sends at least
30. Total Congress: at least 390 people. To pick a president: pick a colony by lot, then the whole Congress votes for
a president from that colony's delegates. Next time, pick from the other 12 colonies, and so on, until everyone's
had a turn. To make a law, you need a three-fifths majority. Anyone who wants to start a fight in a system this
fair is basically siding with Lucifer.

Since this is a delicate start, it should come from a group between the government and the people—a Continental
Conference.

The conference should have 26 members of Congress (2 per colony), 2 members from each Assembly, and 5 representatives
of the people chosen in the capital cities. This combines knowledge (experience) and power (the people's authority).

Their job: write a Continental Charter (like our own Magna Carta). This charter would set the number of members
and how they're chosen, when they meet, and the split between Congress and the Assemblies. Remember: our strength
is continental, not provincial. It must guarantee freedom and property for all, and especially the free exercise
of religion. Once the charter is done, the conference dissolves, and the new government takes over. God preserve
our peace and happiness. Amen.
