# THOUGHTS ON THE CURRENT VIBE OF AMERICAN AFFAIRS.

In the following pages, I'm just dropping straight facts, plain arguments, and common sense. I've got no complex
intro—just a request that you check your biases at the door and let your reason and your feelings decide for
themselves. Step up and embrace the true character of a human; look past today and see the bigger picture.

Whole books have been written on the beef between England and America. People from all walks of life have jumped
into the drama for all sorts of reasons, but nothing has worked, and the time for talking is over. Now, it's about
who can actually hold the line. The King chose the path of violence, and the continent just accepted the challenge.

There was this guy, Mr. Pelham (who was smart but had his flaws), who once got roasted in the House of Commons
because his plans were only temporary. He just replied, "They'll last my time." If that kind of mid, cowardly
mindset takes over the colonies now, future generations will look back at our ancestors with pure disgust.

The sun has never shined on a cause with more main character energy. This isn't about one city, one state, or
one kingdom—this is about a whole continent, at least an eighth of the habitable world. This isn't a "right now"
problem; our kids and their kids are already in this, and they'll be feeling the ripples of what we do now until the
end of time. This is the seed time for continental unity, trust, and honor. Any crack we leave now is like carving
a name into a young oak tree; the scar just grows with the tree until it's a permanent mark for everyone to see.

By switching from arguments to arms, we've entered a whole new era of politics. The way we think has shifted. Any
plans or proposals from before April 19th—before the fighting started—are like last year's calendar: they might
have been right then, but they're useless and outdated now. Everyone on both sides used to agree on one thing:
staying united with Great Britain. The only difference was *how* to do it—one side wanted force, the other wanted
friendship. But force failed, and friendship has left the chat.

People keep talking about the "benefits" of making up, like it's some beautiful dream that just vanished, leaving
us right where we started. It's only fair that we look at the other side: the actual, material damage these colonies
take—and will always take—by staying connected to and dependent on Great Britain. Let's look at this connection
through the lens of nature and common sense to see what we've actually got to trust if we split, and what we're
signing up for if we stay.

Some claim that since America thrived while connected to Great Britain, we need that connection to stay happy. That
logic is a total fail. That's like saying because a baby thrived on milk, it should never eat solid food, or that
the first twenty years of your life are the blueprint for the next twenty. But even that gives Britain too much
credit. I'll say it straight: America would have thrived just as much—probably way more—if no European power
had ever touched it. Our wealth comes from trading the basics of life, and as long as Europe needs to eat, we've
got a market.

"But she protected us!" some say. No, she monopolized us. She defended the continent using our money as well as
hers. She would've defended Turkey for the exact same reason: trade and power.

We've been blinded by old prejudices and sacrificed too much to superstition. We bragged about British "protection"
without realizing their motive was profit, not love. They didn't protect us from our enemies for *our* sake, but
from *their* enemies for *their* own sake. These people have no beef with us—they only hate us because we're
tied to Britain. If Britain stopped claiming the continent, or if we just cut the cord, we'd be chill with France
and Spain even if they were at war with Britain. The disaster of the last war in Hanover should be a warning:
these connections are dangerous.

Parliament recently claimed that the colonies only have a relationship with each other through the "parent
country"—like Pennsylvania and the Jerseys are only sisters because of England. That's a weird way to prove a
relationship, but it's the perfect way to prove an "enemyship." France and Spain aren't our enemies as Americans;
they're our enemies only because we're subjects of Great Britain.

"But Britain is the parent country!" some say. Then that makes her behavior even worse. Even animals don't eat
their own young, and "savages" don't go to war with their own families. If that claim is true, it's just a reason
to shame her. But it's not true—or only barely. The phrase "mother country" was just a clever trick used by
the king and his yes-men to manipulate the naive. Europe, not England, is the parent of America. This new world
has been a sanctuary for people fleeing persecution for civil and religious freedom from all over Europe. They
didn't run to the "tender embrace of a mother," they ran from the cruelty of a monster. And England is the worst
of them—the same tyranny that drove the first settlers away is still chasing their grandkids.

In this massive part of the world, we can forget the tiny borders of England (which is only 360 miles long) and
scale up our friendships. We claim brotherhood with every European Christian and feel proud of that.

It's interesting to see how we get over local bias as we see more of the world. A guy born in an English
town associates most with his neighbors in the same parish. If he meets them a few miles away, he calls them
"townsfolk." If he goes to another county, he calls them "countrymen." But if they meet in France, they just call
each other "Englishmen." By that same logic, all Europeans meeting in America are countrymen. England, Holland,
Germany, or Sweden are just like streets or towns on the larger scale; those distinctions are too small for a
continental mind. Not even a third of the people here are actually of English descent. So calling England the
"mother country" is just fake, selfish, and narrow-minded.

Even if we were all English, so what? Nothing. Britain is now an open enemy, which cancels out any other title. And
saying it's our "duty" to reconcile is a joke. The first king of the current line (William the Conqueror) was
French, and half the Peers of England are descendants of France. By that logic, England should be governed by France.

People brag about the "united strength" of Britain and the colonies, saying they could defy the world together. That's
just pure ego. War is unpredictable. And this continent would never let itself be drained of people just to support
British wars in Asia, Africa, or Europe.

Besides, why do we need to defy the world? Our plan is trade. If we handle that right, we'll have the peace and
friendship of all Europe, because every European country wants America to be a free port. Our trade will be our
protection, and the fact that we don't have mountains of gold and silver will actually keep invaders away.

I challenge anyone who's desperate to reconcile to show me *one single advantage* this continent gets from being
tied to Great Britain. Just one. I'll say it again: zero. Our corn will sell in any European market, and we'll
pay for our imports regardless of where we buy them.

But the damage and disadvantages of that connection are endless. Our duty to the world and to ourselves is to end
the alliance. Any submission to Britain just drags this continent into European wars and fights with nations that
would otherwise be our friends. Since Europe is our market, we shouldn't have a biased connection to any one part
of it. America's true interest is to stay clear of European drama, which she can't do as long as she's just a pawn
in British politics.

Europe is too crowded with kingdoms to ever be at peace. Whenever England fights a foreign power, American trade
gets wrecked because of that connection. The next war might not be like the last one, and if it's not, the people
calling for reconciliation now will be the first ones begging for separation then, because being neutral is a safer
bet than being on a warship. Everything that's right or natural screams for separation. The blood of the dead and
the voice of nature are crying: "It's time to part." Even the distance the Almighty put between England and America
is a natural sign that one was never meant to rule the other. The timing of when the continent was found and how
it was peopled only makes the argument stronger. The Reformation happened right around the discovery of America,
as if God wanted to open a sanctuary for the persecuted when home was no longer safe.

British authority over this continent is a system that has to end sooner or later. Anyone who's thinking seriously
can't find joy in looking forward, knowing that this "present constitution" is just temporary. As parents, we
can't be happy knowing this government won't last long enough to secure anything for our kids. And since we're
basically running the next generation into debt, we owe it to them to fix this now; otherwise, we're treating
them pitifully. To see our duty clearly, we need to imagine ourselves a few years in the future; from that height,
the view is clear, even if present fears and biases are blocking it now.

I don't want to be unnecessarily offensive, but I think everyone pushing for reconciliation fits into a few
categories: the "interested" people who can't be trusted; the weak people who can't see; the biased people who
*won't* see; and the "moderates" who think better of Europe than it deserves. That last group, with their "careful
deliberation," will cause more disasters for this continent than the other three combined.

Many people are lucky enough to live far from the pain; the disaster hasn't hit their door yet, so they don't feel
how unstable all American property actually is. But just imagine for a moment that you're in Boston. That place is
a lesson in wisdom: renounce a power you can't trust. People who were living in luxury a few months ago now have
a choice: stay and starve, or leave and beg. If they stay, their "friends" might burn them out; if they leave,
the soldiers plunder them. They're prisoners with no hope of rescue, and any attempt to save them just exposes
them to the fury of both armies.

People with "passive tempers" shrug off Britain's crimes and hope for the best, saying, "Come on, we'll be friends
again." But check the actual feelings of humans. Put the idea of reconciliation to the test of nature and tell me:
can you actually love, honor, and faithfully serve the power that brought fire and sword into your land? If you
can't, you're just lying to yourself and ruining the future for your kids. Any future connection with a Britain
you can't love or honor will be forced and fake—built on "convenience" and destined to crash even harder than
before. But if you say you can just ignore the violations, then let me ask: Has your house been burned? Has your
property been destroyed right in front of you? Are your wife and kids without a bed or bread? Have you lost a
parent or a child to their hands, and you're the ruined survivor? If not, you're not in a position to judge those
who have. But if you *have* and you can still shake hands with the murderers, then you're unworthy of being called
a husband, father, friend, or lover. Whatever your rank, you have the heart of a coward and the spirit of a suck-up.

This isn't about exaggerating; it's about using the feelings and affections that nature gives us—without which
we couldn't even function in society. I'm not showing you horror just to make you want revenge, but to wake us
up from a cowardly sleep so we can actually move toward a goal. Britain and Europe can't conquer America unless
America conquers herself through delay and fear. This winter is worth a whole age if we use it right, but if we
waste it, the whole continent suffers. Anyone who sacrifices this precious time deserves the worst punishment.

It's against reason, against the order of the world, and against every historical example to think this continent can
stay subject to an outside power. Even the most optimistic people in Britain don't believe it. No amount of "wisdom"
can create a plan other than separation that can guarantee the continent even a year of security. Reconciliation
is a fake dream. Nature has quit the connection, and "Art" can't replace her. As Milton said, "never can true
reconcilement grow where wounds of deadly hate have pierced so deep."

Every peaceful method has failed. Our prayers were rejected with disdain and only served to show that nothing feeds
a king's ego or stubbornness more than repeated begging. That's exactly how the kings of Europe became absolute:
look at Denmark and Sweden. Since nothing but blows will work, for God's sake, let's just separate. Don't leave
the next generation to cut throats under the fake names of "parent" and "child."

Saying they'll never try it again is delusional. We thought that when the stamp-act was repealed, but a year or
two later we were proven wrong. It's like thinking a nation that's been defeated once will never start a fight again.

When it comes to governing, Britain can't do this continent justice. The job will soon be too big and too messy to
be handled by a power so far away and so ignorant of us. If they can't conquer us, they can't govern us. Running
three or four thousand miles with a petition, waiting months for an answer, and then waiting months more for an
explanation is just childish. It was fine once, but it's time to stop.

Small islands that can't protect themselves are the right kind of place for kingdoms to take over. But it's absurd
to think a continent should be governed by an island. In nature, a satellite is never larger than its primary
planet. England and America reverse that order, which proves they belong to different systems: England to Europe,
America to itself.

I'm not pushing for separation out of pride or resentment. I'm clearly, positively, and conscientiously convinced
that it's the only way for this continent to survive. Anything else is just patchwork that won't last. It's leaving
the sword to our children and shrinking back just when a little more effort would make this continent the glory
of the earth.

Since Britain hasn't shown the slightest interest in a real compromise, we can be sure that no deal will be worth
the blood and treasure we've already spent.

The goal should always be proportional to the cost. Removing a few hated officials is not worth the millions
we've spent. A temporary trade stop was an inconvenience, but it would've been worth it if we'd gotten the
laws repealed. But if the whole continent has to go to war—if every man has to be a soldier—it's not worth
fighting just to get rid of a pathetic ministry. We're paying a "Bunker-hill price" for a law. I've always seen
the independence of this continent as inevitable. With the continent maturing so fast, the event is close. Once
the fighting started, it wasn't worth arguing about something that time would have fixed anyway—unless we were
serious. Otherwise, it's like spending a fortune on a lawsuit to fix a tenant's mistakes right when their lease is
expiring. I was the biggest fan of reconciliation before April 19th, 1775, but the moment I heard what happened
that day, I rejected the sullen, hardened Pharaoh of England forever. I despise the wretch who calls himself the
"father of his people" while he calmly sleeps with their blood on his soul.

But let's say things were "made up." What then? The ruin of the continent. Here's why:

First, the power to govern stays with the king, so he has a veto over everything. He's already shown he's an enemy of
liberty and craves absolute power. Is he the right person to tell us, "You can only make laws that I like"? Anyone
in America knows that under the "present constitution," we can't make any laws without the king's permission. He'll
never allow any law that doesn't serve his own purpose. We could be just as enslaved by a *lack* of laws as by
laws made in England. After things are "made up," the crown will do everything to keep us low and humble. Instead
of moving forward, we'll go backward or spend our time in ridiculous petitions. We're already bigger than the king
wants us to be—do you think he'll try to make us smaller? Let's get to the point: Is a power that's jealous of
our success the right power to govern us? If you say "No," you're an independant. Independence just means: do we
make our own laws, or does the king—the biggest enemy this continent has—tell us "no laws unless I like them"?

"But the king has a veto in England too!" you say. In terms of logic and order, it's ridiculous that a 21-year-old
(which has happened) can tell millions of older, wiser people, "I forbid this act from being law." I won't spend too
much time on that absurdity, but I'll say this: the king lives in England, and we don't. That makes it a different
case. His veto here is ten times more dangerous than in England, because in England he'd never veto a bill that
strengthened England's defense, but in America, he'd never let such a bill pass.

America is just a side-project in British politics. England only cares about us as far as it helps them. Therefore,
they'll crush our growth any time it doesn't help them or gets in their way. Imagine being under a "second-hand
government" like that! People don't stop being enemies just because you change a name. To show you why reconciliation
is dangerous: the king might actually repeal the acts right now just to trick us into letting him back in. He'd rather
use craft and subtilty in the long run than force in the short run. Reconciliation and ruin are almost the same thing.

Secondly, even the best terms we could get would just be a temporary fix—a kind of "guardianship" that ends when
the colonies come of age. In the meantime, everything will be unstable. Wealthy emigrants won't come to a country
where the government is hanging by a thread and could collapse into chaos any day. Many current residents would
use the gap to sell their stuff and leave the continent.

But the strongest argument of all is that only independence—a continental government—can keep the peace and
stop civil wars. I'm terrified of reconciling with Britain now, because it'll probably just lead to a revolt
somewhere else, which could be way more fatal than Britain's malice.

Thousands are already ruined by British brutality; thousands more will follow. Those people feel differently
than those of us who haven't suffered. All they have left is liberty; everything else they had was sacrificed for
it. They have nothing left to lose, so they won't submit. Plus, the general mood of the colonies toward a British
government is like a teenager who's almost grown up: they just don't care about her anymore. A government that can't
keep the peace isn't a government at all. Why pay for something that doesn't work? What can Britain actually do if
a civil riot breaks out the day after reconciliation? Her power would just be on paper. Some people say they fear
independence will cause civil wars. They're wrong. There's ten times more to fear from a patched-up connection
than from independence. I put myself in the shoes of the sufferers: if I were driven from my home, my property
destroyed, and my life ruined, I could never vibe with the idea of reconciliation.

The colonies have shown such a spirit of order and obedience to the continental government that any reasonable
person should be happy. Any fear that one colony will try to dominate another is just childish and ridiculous.

When there's no distinction, there's no superiority. Perfect equality removes a temptation. The republics of
Europe are generally at peace. Holland and Switzerland don't have wars, foreign or domestic. Monarchies, however,
are never at rest. The crown itself tempts thugs at home, and the ego that comes with royal power always leads to
fights with foreign powers—fights that a republican government, based on natural principles, would avoid.

If there's any real reason to fear independence, it's just because we don't have a plan yet. People can't see
the way out. So, as a starting point, I offer these hints—though I'm humble about them; they might just be the
spark for something better. If we collect the scattered thoughts of individuals, smart people can turn them into
something useful.

Let the assemblies be annual, with only a President. Make representation more equal. Their business should be
purely domestic and subject to a Continental Congress.

Divide each colony into six, eight, or ten districts. Each district sends delegates to Congress, so each colony
sends at least thirty. The total number in Congress will be at least 390. Each Congress sits and chooses a president
this way: pick one colony by lot, then have the whole Congress elect a president from that colony's delegates. In
the next Congress, pick from the other twelve, rotating until all thirteen have had a turn. To make sure no law
is passed unless it's actually just, a three-fifths majority is required. Anyone who wants to create discord in
a system this equal is basically joining Lucifer's revolt.

Because this is a delicate process, it should start with an intermediate body between the governors and the
people. Let's have a Continental Conference.

A committee of 26 members of Congress (two from each colony), two members from each House of Assembly or Provincial
Convention, and five representatives of the people chosen in the capital city of each province by whoever wants
to vote. In this conference, you get the two big things: knowledge and power. The experienced members provide the
counsel, and the people's reps provide the legal authority.

Once they meet, their job is to write a Continental Charter—like a Magna Carta for the United Colonies. It
should set the number and the way we choose members of Congress and Assembly, their meeting dates, and the line
between their duties. Remember: our strength is continental, not provincial. Secure freedom and property for all,
and above all, the free exercise of religion according to conscience. Once that's done, the Conference dissolves,
and the new bodies chosen by the charter become the legislators and governors of this continent. May God preserve
our peace and happiness. Amen.

If anyone is appointed for this, I'll give them a tip from that wise observer Dragonetti: "The science of the
politician consists in fixing the true point of happiness and freedom. Those men would deserve the gratitude of
ages who should discover a mode of government that contained the greatest sum of individual happiness with the
least national expense."

Dragonetti on virtue and rewards.

"But where is the King of America?" you ask. I'll tell you, friend: He reigns above, and He doesn't wreck
humanity like the Royal Brute of Britain. But since we still like earthly honors, let's set a day to proclaim
the charter. Place the charter on the divine law—the word of God. Put a crown on it so the world knows that in
America, the Law is King. In absolute governments, the King is law; in free countries, the Law ought to be King,
and nothing else. Then, to make sure no one abuses it, demolish the crown at the end of the ceremony and scatter
it among the people—because it's their right.

Having our own government is our natural right. When you think about how unpredictable life is, it's way wiser
and safer to build our own constitution now, while we can, than to leave it to chance. If we don't do it now,
some "Massanello" might rise up, use popular anger to gather the desperate and discontented, and sweep away the
liberties of the continent like a flood. If America goes back to Britain, the instability will be a temptation for
some adventurer to try his luck. Britain couldn't help us in time; we'd be suffering just like the Britons did
under the Conqueror. You who oppose independence now don't know what you're doing; you're leaving the door open
for eternal tyranny by leaving the seat of government empty. Thousands upon thousands would find it glorious to
kick out that barbaric, hellish power that stirred up the Indians and Negroes to destroy us. That cruelty is a
double crime: it's brutal to us and treacherous to them.

Talking about "friendship" with people we can't trust—and whose actions have wounded us in a thousand places—is
just madness. Every day that passes kills the last remains of any kinship. Why hope that as the relationship dies,
the affection grows? Why think we'll get along better when we have ten times more and bigger things to fight over?

You who talk of harmony and reconciliation: can you give us back the time we lost? Can you make a prostitute innocent
again? You can't reconcile Britain and America. The last cord is broken; the people of England are already speaking
out against us. There are injuries that nature cannot forgive; if she did, she wouldn't be nature. A lover can't
forgive the person who raped his mistress, and the continent can't forgive the murders committed by Britain. The
Almighty put these feelings in us for a reason. They are the guardians of His image in our hearts. They're what
separate us from common animals. The social contract would dissolve and justice would vanish if we were numb to
affection. Robbers and murderers would get away with it if the pain we feel didn't drive us toward justice.

O you who love humanity! You who dare to oppose not just the tyranny, but the tyrant: step up! Every spot in
the old world is overrun by oppression. Freedom has been hunted across the globe. Asia and Africa kicked her out
long ago. Europe treats her like a stranger, and England has told her to leave. Receive the fugitive, and build
a sanctuary for all of mankind.
