# INTRODUCTION

Maybe the vibes in these pages aren't "trendy" enough yet to be popular. When people spend forever not questioning
something, it starts to look right on the surface, and they'll throw a fit to protect "how things have always
been." But the drama always dies down. Time converts more people than logic does.

When power is abused for way too long and way too hard, people start questioning if that power should even exist
(even in things they wouldn't have cared about if they weren't being pushed to the edge). Since the King of
England has decided to back the Parliament in what he calls "their rights," and since the people here are getting
absolutely wrecked by this duo, we have every right to question where they get their authority and reject both of
them for overstepping.

In this text, I've stayed away from personal tea. No shoutouts, no call-outs. The people who are actually based
don't need a pamphlet to feel validated; and those who are tripping or being hater will eventually stop on their
own, as long as we don't waste too much energy trying to "fix" them.

America's struggle is basically humanity's struggle. A lot of what's happening isn't just local—it's
universal. Anyone who actually cares about people should be pressed about this. Burning a whole country down
and declaring war on the basic natural rights of everyone is something that should bother anyone with a heart,
regardless of their political team.

— THE AUTHOR

P.S. This new edition was delayed because I wanted to see if anyone would actually try to debunk the "Independence"
take. Since nobody has replied yet, I'm assuming they've got nothing. It's been way too long for a proper response
to be ready.

Who wrote this doesn't actually matter. The focus should be on the ideas, not the person. But just so you know:
I'm not riding for any party, and I'm not being paid or influenced by anyone—just logic and principles.

Philly, Feb 14, 1776
