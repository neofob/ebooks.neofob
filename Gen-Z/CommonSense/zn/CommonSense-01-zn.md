# INTRODUCTION

Look, these takes might not be trending yet. We've been gaslit for so long into thinking the status quo is a vibe
that calling it out feels wrong at first. People will scream "tradition!" to protect the way things are. But the
noise always dies down. Logic is great, but time is what actually wakes people up.

When a power trip goes too far and gets violent, it forces everyone to ask: "Wait, who actually gave you the right
to do this?" (And honestly, we wouldn't even be asking if the pain hadn't become unbearable). The King of England
is out here backing Parliament's "rights" while they both crush the people. We have every right to check their
receipts and cancel their stolen authority.

I've kept this strictly about the ideas. No personal beef, no clout-chasing compliments, no dragging individuals. The
people who actually matter don't need a pamphlet to prove their worth, and the ones tripping will eventually stop
on their own if we don't waste our energy trying to "fix" them.

The struggle in America isn't just a local glitch—it's a fight for all of humanity. Things are happening here
that hit on a universal level, touching the core of what it means to be human. When you burn a country to the ground
and declare war on the basic rights we were born with, it's not "politics"—it's a crisis for anyone with a heart,
regardless of who they vote for.

— THE AUTHOR

P.S. This new edition was on hold just in case someone actually tried to debunk the independence vibe. But since
the silence has been deafening and the deadline for a real response has passed, it's safe to assume nobody can
actually argue against it.

Who I am doesn't matter. The focus is the message, not the messenger. But for the record: I'm not a corporate shill,
I'm not playing for any team, and I'm not under anyone's thumb. I only answer to reason and principle.

Philadelphia, February 14, 1776
