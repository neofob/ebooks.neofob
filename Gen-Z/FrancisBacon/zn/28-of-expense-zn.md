# OF EXPENSE (THE BUDGETING GUIDE)

Riches are made for spending—specifically for honor and doing good. But "extraordinary" spending should be
limited by how important the occasion is. "Voluntary undoing" (spending a lot for a cause) can be a win for your
country or your soul. But "ordinary" spending should be limited by your actual bank account. You have to manage
it so that your bills are lower than what people *think* you're spending.

Pro tip: if you want to stay stable, your ordinary expenses should be half of your income. If you actually want
to get rich, keep it to a third. It's not "low-tier" for the most powerful people to actually look at their own
finances. Some avoid it because they're scared they'll find out they're broke, but you can't heal a wound without
searching for it first.

If you can't track your own money, you need to pick your employees carefully and change them often. New people
are more scared of getting caught and less "subtle" (shady). If you can only check your finances occasionally,
put everything into "certainties" (safe investments).

Balance your spending: if you're spending a lot on food, save on clothes. If you're spending a lot on the "main
hall," save on the stables. If you're "plentiful" in every category, you're going to crash.

When cleaning up your estate, don't be too sudden, but don't wait too long. Hasty selling is just as bad as paying
too much interest. Also, if you clear all your debts at once, you'll probably just relapse into your old habits. It's
better to clear them by degrees—this builds a "habit of frugality" and helps both your mind and your money.

If you're repairing your finances, don't ignore the small things. It's less embarrassing to cut "petty charges"
than to stoop to "petty earnings." Be careful about starting new expenses that will continue forever; but for
one-time things, you can be more magnificent.
