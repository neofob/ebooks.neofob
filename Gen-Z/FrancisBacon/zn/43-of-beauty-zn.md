# OF BEAUTY (THE AESTHETIC VIBE)

Virtue is like a high-end gemstone—it looks best in a simple setting. Virtue shines most in a body that's
"comely" (pleasant) but not necessarily "delicate." It's more about a "dignified presence" than "perfect
features." Interestingly, the most beautiful people aren't always the most virtuous. It's almost as if nature
spends all its energy on the "visuals" and forgets to put in the work for the soul. They end up being "accomplished"
(good at the basics) but lack a "great spirit"—they care more about how they act than who they are.

But there are exceptions. Augustus Caesar, Philip le Bel, and others were both high-spirit and top-tier beautiful.

In the world of beauty:
- "Favor" (general appeal) is more important than "color."
- "Decent and gracious motion" is more important than "favor." This is the best part of beauty because a picture
can't capture it, and you can't even see it at first glance.

True beauty often has some "strangeness" in its proportions. Some painters try to use "geometric proportions"
and others "mix and match" the best parts of different faces. But those "perfect" faces usually please no one but
the painter. Real beauty is a "felicity"—like a great piece of music—it can't be done by a rulebook. You can
have a face where every single part is "mid," but altogether, the vibe is just right.

Because "decent motion" is so key, older people can often seem more amiable—"the autumn of the beautiful is
beautiful." Youth is only comely when you "pardon" their rough edges and imagine how they'll look when they mature.

Beauty is like "summer fruit"—it's easy to spoil and doesn't last. It often leads to a "dissolute" (messy) youth
and an "out of countenance" (embarrassed) old age. But when it fits well, it makes a person's virtues shine and
their vices blush.
