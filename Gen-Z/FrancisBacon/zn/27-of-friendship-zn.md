# OF FRIENDSHIP (THE REALEST BOND)

Someone once said, "Whoever loves solitude is either a wild beast or a god." That's a wild mix of truth and
cap. It's true that a secret hatred for society is a "beast" vibe. But it's a total lie that it's "divine,"
unless you're sequestering yourself for a higher conversation—like the old hermits and holy fathers did. Most
people don't even get what solitude actually is. Being in a crowd isn't "company," and seeing faces is just like
looking at a gallery of pictures, and talking is just noise if there's no love. There's a Latin saying: "A big
city is a big solitude." In a huge town, friends are scattered, and you don't get that real fellowship you find
in smaller neighborhoods. But let's be real: the most miserable solitude of all is wanting true friends and not
having them. Without them, the world is just a wilderness. If you're just not built for friendship, you're taking
after the beasts, not humans.

The first "fruit" of friendship is the emotional relief—the "discharge" of the heart's pressure. We know that
blockages in the body are dangerous, and it's the same for the mind. You can take medicine for your liver, spleen,
or lungs, but the only thing that opens the heart is a true friend. They're the ones you can tell your griefs,
joys, fears, hopes, and suspicions to—it's like a "civil confession."

It's wild how much high-status kings and monarchs value this. They'll literally risk their own safety and power
just to have a real friend. Because of the gap between a prince and his subjects, they can't just "find" a friend;
they have to "raise" someone up to be their equal. The modern world calls these people "favorites," but the Romans
called them "participes curarum"—partners in their cares. This is the real bond. Even the most strategic and
cold princes did this.

Take Sylla and Pompey. Sylla raised Pompey so high that Pompey eventually told him to be quiet, saying that more
people adore the sun rising than the sun setting. Or Julius Caesar and Decimus Brutus—Brutus had so much influence
that he could literally lead Caesar to his death just by gently lifting him by the arm. Augustus and Agrippa were
another pair; Agrippa became so powerful that he basically told Augustus he had to marry his daughter or die. There
was no third option.

These princes might have had wives, kids, and nephews, but none of that could replace the comfort of a true
friend. As a historian noted about Duke Charles the Hardy, he kept his secrets to himself, and that "closeness"
eventually wrecked his mind. Pythagoras had a dark but true parable: "Do not eat the heart." People who don't have
friends to open up to are basically "cannibals of their own hearts."

The best part? Sharing yourself with a friend does two opposite things: it doubles your joys and cuts your griefs
in half. It's like the alchemist's stone for the mind—it turns everything into a win. In nature, union strengthens
action and dulls a violent hit; the same happens with minds.

The second "fruit" of friendship is for the brain. Friendship doesn't just clear the emotional storms; it brings
"daylight" to the understanding. When you're stuck with a million thoughts, talking it out with a friend clarifies
and breaks them up. You organize your thoughts better, you see how they sound in words, and you actually become
"wiser than yourself." An hour of talking is better than a day of thinking. As Themistocles said, speech is like
a tapestry—it opens up the image, whereas thoughts are just "packed away." Even if your friend can't give you
direct advice, just relating your thoughts to them keeps you from "smothering" in your own head.

And then there's the "faithful counsel" of a friend. A friend's light is "drier and purer" than your own judgment,
which is usually drenched in your own biases and customs. There's no bigger flatterer than your own ego, and the
only remedy is the honesty of a friend. For your personal life, a friend's "call-out" is the best medicine. Many
high-status people make huge, absurd mistakes just because they don't have a friend to tell them they're being
cringe. In business, thinking you're "all in all" is a fail. Two eyes see more than one. And don't just get
"scattered" advice from different people; that'll just distract you. You need a friend who knows your "whole estate"
so they don't accidentally fix one thing and break another.

The last "fruit" of friendship is basically "backup." A friend is like a second version of yourself. We all have
things we can't do ourselves—maybe we run out of time. If you have a true friend, you can trust that your goals
and works will continue after you're gone. You basically have "two lives." Also, there are things you can't say
or do with "face" (modesty). You can't brag about your own merits, but a friend can do it for you. You can't
always speak to your son as a father or your wife as a husband—but a friend can speak whatever the situation
requires. If you don't have a friend, you might as well leave the stage.
