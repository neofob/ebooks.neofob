# OF DEATH (THE FINAL LOGOUT)

People fear death the same way kids fear the dark; and just like that kind of fear gets worse with scary stories,
so does the fear of the end. Now, thinking about death as the price of our mistakes and the portal to another world
is holy and deep; but fearing it just because it's a natural part of the biology is kinda weak. Even in religious
vibes, there's sometimes a mix of vanity and superstition. You'll read in some of those old monk books that you
should imagine the pain of having just your fingertip tortured to guess what death feels like when the whole body
dissolves. But honestly, death often hits with less pain than a limb torture, because the most vital parts aren't
even that sensitive.

As a philosopher put it: "The hype around death is scarier than death itself." The groans, the shaking, the pale
face, the crying friends, the black clothes, and the funerals—that's what makes death look like a nightmare. But
it's interesting that almost any strong emotion in the human mind can outmuscle the fear of death. Death isn't
such a terrifying enemy when you've got so many other vibes that can win the fight. Revenge beats death; love just
ignores it; honor chases it; grief runs toward it; and fear gets there first. We even read that after Emperor Otho
ended it all, pity (the softest of all vibes) made many people die just out of compassion for their leader—the
ultimate loyalty. Seneca even added that some people just get bored. You could want to log off not because you're
brave or miserable, but just because you're tired of doing the same thing every single day.

It's also worth noting how little the approach of death changes people who are truly chill. They stay the same
person until the very last second. Augustus Caesar died giving a compliment: "Livia, remember our marriage,
live and be well." Tiberius died acting for the cameras. Vespasian died with a joke, sitting on his stool:
"I think I'm becoming a god." Galba died with a quote, offering his neck: "Strike, if it's for the good of the
Roman people." Septimus Severus died in a rush: "Get over here, if there's anything left for me to do."

The Stoics spent way too much energy prepping for death, and by making such a big deal out of it, they actually
made it seem scarier. It's better to see it as one of nature's final gifts. Dying is just as natural as being born;
to a newborn baby, maybe both are equally stressful. Someone who dies while chasing a goal is like someone wounded
in the heat of battle—they barely feel the hit. A mind locked into something good can block out the pain of the
end. But above all, the sweetest song is the "Nunc dimittis," when a person has finally secured their wins and
met their expectations. Death also does this: it opens the door to a legacy and kills the envy. Once you're gone,
everyone loves you.
