# OF NOBILITY (THE LEGACY FLEX)

Let's talk about nobility—first as a part of how a state is run, then as a trait of individuals. A monarchy with
zero nobility is basically a pure tyranny (like the Turks). Nobility creates a balance of power and draws the
people's attention away from the royal line. But democracies don't need it; they're usually more chill and have
fewer riots because people focus on the *work* being done, not the *person* doing it—or if they look at the
person, it's just to see if they're the best fit for the job, not because of their family tree. The Swiss do it
well despite their differences, because their bond is "utility" (usefulness), not "respects." The Dutch are also
great at this; when there's equality, decisions are more fair and people are happier to pay their taxes.

A powerful nobility adds "majesty" to a king but shrinks his actual power. It puts energy into the people but
crushes their finances. It's a win when nobles aren't too big to challenge the king or the law, but are still high
enough to act as a "buffer" so that the anger of the lower classes hits them before it hits the king. But having
too many nobles just makes a state poor—it's an expensive overhead. Plus, when a noble family loses its money,
it creates a weird gap between their "honor" and their actual bank account.

As for nobility in individuals, it's a vibe to see an ancient castle that hasn't fallen apart, or a massive
tree that's still healthy. Even more so to see an ancient noble family that has survived the waves of time. New
nobility is just a "power move," but ancient nobility is the "act of time." Those who are first raised to nobility
are usually more virtuous (they had to hustle) but less "innocent" (they probably did some shady things to get
there). But the memory of their wins stays with their kids, while their fails die with them.

Birth-nobility often kills the hustle. Someone who doesn't have to work envies the one who does. Also, nobles
can't really "move up" anymore, so they often get salty when others are rising. On the other hand, being noble
stops others from envying you as much, because you already have the "honor" badge. Kings who have capable nobles
find it much easier to get things done, because people naturally bend to them—they were basically "born to lead."
