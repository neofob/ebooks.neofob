# OF DELAYS (THE ART OF THE TIMING)

Fortune is like the stock market: sometimes, if you can just hold a bit longer, the price will drop. Other times,
it's like a limited-time offer—it's full at first, then it starts disappearing piece by piece while the price
stays high. As the saying goes, "Opportunity turns a bald head"—meaning if you don't grab it when the "hair" (the
opportunity) is right in front of you, you're left with nothing. The ultimate wisdom is knowing how to time your moves.

Dangers aren't necessarily "light" just because they seem light at first. More people have been deceived by "easy"
dangers than forced by "hard" ones. It's actually better to deal with some dangers halfway, even if they aren't
close yet, than to watch them for too long—because if you watch too long, you'll eventually fall asleep. On the
flip side, don't overreact to "long shadows" (like seeing an enemy's shadow when the moon is low) and attack too
early. Or don't "buckle up" too early and signal your move to the enemy.

The key is weighing the "ripeness" of the occasion. Use "Argus" (the hundred-eyed giant) for the beginning—meaning
watch everything carefully. Then use "Briareus" (the hundred-handed giant) for the end—meaning execute with total
speed. The "helmet of Pluto" (the invisibility cloak) for a politician is: secrecy in the council, and celerity
(speed) in the execution. Once you move to the execution phase, nothing is more invisible than pure speed—like
a bullet in the air that moves faster than the eye can see.
