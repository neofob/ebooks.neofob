# OF FOLLOWERS AND FRIENDS (THE SQUAD DYNAMIC)

Having "costly" followers is a mid move. It doesn't just drain your wallet; it drains your energy because they're
always "importuning" (begging) for things. Ordinary followers should only expect the basics: your "countenance"
(support), recommendations, and protection.

The "toxic" types:
- **Factious followers:** People who follow you not because they like you, but because they hate someone else. This
leads to "bad intelligence" and drama between leaders.
- **"Glorious" followers:** People who act like your hype-men. They're a liability because they can't keep a secret
and they turn your success into "envy" from others.
- **Espials:** Followers who are actually spies. They dig for secrets in the house and trade them to others. They
often get favor because they're "officious" (always providing "info").

The "based" way:
The most honorable way to lead is to be a "talent scout"—someone who helps virtuous and deserving people rise,
regardless of their status. If there's no huge difference in skill, it's better to take the "passable" person over
the "over-qualified" one. In a crisis, "active" people are more useful than "virtuous" ones.

In government:
- Treat people of the same rank equally to avoid making some "insolent" and others "discontent."
- But in "favor" (who you actually like), be selective. This makes the preferred people more thankful and the
others more eager to please you.
- Don't "over-hype" someone too early—you can't maintain that level of favor forever.
- Being "governed by one" person is risky—it makes you look soft and opens you up to scandal. But being "distracted
by many" is worse—you'll just end up being a puppet of the last person who talked to you.

The best move: take advice from a few true friends. "Lookers-on" (observers) often see more than the "gamesters"
actually playing the game.

The sad truth: there's very little real friendship in the world, especially between equals. Most "friendships"
are actually between a superior and an inferior, where the higher-status person can actually "comprehend"
(support/protect) the other.
