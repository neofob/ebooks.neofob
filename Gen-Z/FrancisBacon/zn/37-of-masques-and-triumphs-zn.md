# OF MASQUES AND TRIUMPHS (THE LUXURY FLEX)

These things are basically just "toys" compared to the serious stuff, but since princes love them, it's better
they be "elegant" than just "expensive."

Dancing to song is a high-status vibe. The song should be performed by a choir placed high up, with some "broken
music" (complex rhythms), and the lyrics should fit the theme. Acting in song—especially dialogues—is a total
flex. I'm talking about *acting*, not just dancing (which is a bit mid/vulgar). The voices should be strong and
manly (bass and tenor, no high-pitch trebles), and the mood should be tragic, not "dainty." Having multiple choirs
facing each other and doing "catch" songs is a major win.

Turning dances into "figures" (patterns) is just a childish whim. The key is to use things that "naturally hit
the senses," not just "petty wonderments."

The transitions between scenes should be quiet and smooth—that's where the beauty is. It lets the eye reset
before it's full of the same object again. Use lots of light, especially colored and varied lights. The masquers
should have some "teaser" motions on the scene before they actually come down—it makes the audience crave to
see them. The music should be loud, cheerful, and sharp—no "chirping or puling."

For colors under candlelight: white, carnation, and a specific "sea-water green" look best. Using "ouches" or
"spangs" (sequins/glitter) is cheap but looks glorious. Expensive embroidery is actually a waste because you
can't even tell it's there. The costumes should be graceful and fit the person's actual look once the masks are
off—don't just copy "known" outfits like Turks or soldiers.

"Anti-masques" (the opening acts) shouldn't be too long. They usually feature fools, satyrs, baboons, wild men,
witches, a few "turquets," nymphs, or moving statues. Angels aren't funny enough for anti-masques, and things that
are "hideous" (like devils or giants) are too much. The music for these parts should be "recreative" with strange
changes. Adding some "sweet odors" (scents) that suddenly fill the room is a great touch, especially in a hot,
steamy room. Double masques (one for men, one for ladies) add variety. But none of this matters if the room isn't
kept clean and neat.

For the "justs" and "tourneys" (knightly competitions), the glory is in the chariots. Using "strange beasts" like
lions, bears, or camels to pull the chariots is a huge flex. The entrance devices, the bravery of the liveries,
and the furniture of the horses and armor are the highlights.

But anyway, enough of these toys.
