# ON DEATH (THE FINAL LOGOUT - PART 2)

I've thought about death a lot, and honestly, it's the "least of all evils." Everything that's already happened
is like a dream. And if you're hoping for the future, you're just "dreaming while awake." We're essentially dying
every day from the moment we're born.

Physicians use the word "death" to cover all kinds of sorrow, disease, and calamity. But we deal with those things
every hour, so we're already used to "dying."

Some "wise men" fear death because the "change" is bitter. But I don't think anyone actually fears *being
dead*—they just fear the "stroke" of death. I have hope that if nature just renews my "lease" for 21 more years,
I'll be fine. Virtue is about "contemning" (ignoring) the things people fear.

Why be "in love" with your life? If you're just "drowned in security," you're already dead. Your soul is buried,
and your "good angel" is asleep. The only thing in the world that actually matters is a true friend. That freedom
gives me peace—I don't mourn the end that *must* happen, and I don't wish for a single extra minute.

The "rich and powerful" are the ones who fear death the most. In hell, the only way to tell a king from a regular
person is that the kings cry louder—they're just mourning the "good days" and the "wealth" they had to leave
behind. They were so focused on their "blessings" that they forgot the "Hand" that gave them.

"Memento Mori" (Remember you must die). If we keep this in mind, we can enjoy life as people who are "prepared
to lose." The soul is like a great musician trapped in a "defective instrument" (the body). When the body dies,
the soul finally gets to show its wonders.

Death is just an "alteration" of being. But for some, it's an "importunate guest" who won't take "no" for an answer. The
"citizens" (the rich) fear death most because they usually "die intestate" (without a will). They think that by
not making a will, they can "scare destiny" and live longer. They're just trying to anchor themselves to their treasure.

But for some, death is a "redeemer." For the prisoner, the widow, the deposed king, or the broken spirit, the
grave is just a place for "retiredness and rest."

For the usurer, death is a "doleful messenger." He only thinks of death when rumors of war start, and he realizes his
"monuments of coin" can't save him. Some are so desperate that they'll hang themselves in their study, surrounded
by their wealth, just so they can write a will and "depute themselves as their own heir."

I'm not a Stoic who thinks grief is "nothing." But I agree with Caesar that the "suddenest passage" is the easiest. A
"quiet conscience" is the best way to be ready. The worst thing in the world is "deserved evil fame"—following
your own reputation to its funeral while you're still alive.

Death is a friend. If you're not ready to entertain him, you're "not at home." I don't want to "foreflow the tide"
(die too early), but I don't want to live forever either. Old age is just a "return to infancy." I just hope I
die after my enemies (so they can't strip me before I'm cold) and before my friends.

The night is coming. My eyes are closing. I'm about to be as happy for a few hours of sleep as I would be if I
died the first hour I was born.
