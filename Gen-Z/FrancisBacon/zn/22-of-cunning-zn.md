# OF CUNNING (THE SIDE-QUEST STRATEGIES)

People think "cunning" is just "crooked wisdom," but there's a big difference between a cunning man and a wise
man—not just in honesty, but in actual ability. Some people know how to "pack the cards" (cheat) but can't
actually play the game. Some are great at "networking" and "faction wars" but are otherwise mid.

It's one thing to understand people's "humors" (personalities) and another to understand the actual business. People
who study people more than books are great for "practice" (the hustle) but bad for "counsel" (the strategy). They're
like "haberdashers of small wares"—they have a lot of little tricks but no big vision.

Here are some "pro tips" for the cunning:
1. **The Eye Contact:** Watch the person you're talking to with your eyes, but occasionally look down in a "humble"
way. This is a Jesuit move to make the other person feel more comfortable while you read them.
2. **The Distraction:** If you want a quick "yes" on something, distract the person with a different conversation
so they aren't "awake" enough to make objections. One secretary to Queen Elizabeth always did this—he'd get her
talking about state business first so she'd just sign the bills without thinking.
3. **The Rush:** Move things when the other person is in a hurry and doesn't have time to think.
4. **The "Fake Support":** If you want to kill a project that you know someone else is going to propose perfectly,
pretend you want it to happen and then propose it in a way that makes it fail.
5. **The Cliffhanger:** Stop talking in the middle of a point. It makes the other person desperate to know more.
6. **The Bait:** Act differently than usual (change your "visage") so the other person asks "what's wrong?" This
lets you bring up a topic without it seeming like you're the one who wanted to talk about it.
7. **The "Ice Breaker":** For awkward or unpleasant topics, let someone with "less weight" (lower status) start
the conversation, then let the higher-status person come in "by chance" to finish it.
8. **The "World Says":** If you don't want to be seen as the source of a rumor, say "the world says" or "there's
a rumor going around."
9. **The Postscript:** Put the most important part of a letter in the P.S., as if it's just a side-note.
10. **The "I Forgot" Move:** Pass over the most important point of your speech, then come back to it later as if
you almost forgot it.
11. **The "Caught" Move:** Let yourself be "surprised" with a letter in your hand or doing something unusual,
so the other person asks about it and you can "accidentally" tell them what you want them to know.
12. **The "Plant":** Use phrases in your own name that you want *others* to start using. Two guys competing for
a secretary job did this—one said being a secretary in a "declining monarchy" was a risky move. The other guy
repeated those exact words to his friends. The first guy made sure the Queen heard it, and she was so offended by
the "declining monarchy" comment that she banned the other guy.
13. **The "Cat in the Pan":** Tell someone a piece of tea, but make it sound like *someone else* told it to you.
14. **The Negative Flex:** Attack someone by saying what you *don't* do (e.g., "I'm not just looking out for my
own safety").
15. **The Story Wrapper:** Wrap your insinuations in a story. It protects you and makes the other person enjoy the
"tea" more.
16. **The Guided Answer:** Phrase your question so that the answer you want is already built into the options.
17. **The Long Game:** Be patient. Spend a long time talking around a subject before finally hitting the main point.
18. **The Shock Question:** A sudden, bold, unexpected question can "open" a person up. Like when someone who
changed their name was suddenly called by their real name—they instinctively looked back.

These "small wares" are infinite, but it's a tragedy when "cunning men" are mistaken for "wise men" in a state. Some
people just know the "stairs and entries" of business but have no "fair room" (no deep understanding). They find a
"loophole" in the conclusion but can't actually debate the matter. The biggest fail is building your success on
"tricks" instead of "sound proceedings." As Solomon said: the prudent man watches his own steps; the fool looks
for tricks.
