# OF SUPERSTITION (THE CRINGE VERSION OF FAITH)

Honestly, it's better to have no opinion of God at all than to have an opinion that's totally unworthy of him. One is
just unbelief, but the other is a straight-up insult. Superstition is basically just roasting the Deity. Plutarch
put it perfectly: "I'd rather people say Plutarch never existed than say there was a Plutarch who ate his own
children as soon as they were born." Since the insult is bigger towards God, the danger to humans is even bigger.

Atheism just leaves you with your senses, philosophy, natural vibes, laws, and your rep—all of which can guide
you to be a decent person even without religion. But superstition deletes all of that and sets up an absolute
monarchy in your mind. That's why atheism never really crashed states—it just makes people more cautious of
themselves. The era of Augustus Caesar was an "atheist" time, but it was a civil time. Superstition, however,
has wrecked countless states by introducing a new "prime mover" that hijacks the whole government.

The "boss" of superstition is the crowd. In a superstitious world, wise people follow fools, and logic is applied
in reverse. Some religious leaders once said that the "schoolmen" (the academic theologians) were like astronomers
who made up weird "epicycles" and orbital glitches just to make the data fit their theories—even though they
knew those things didn't exist. Similarly, the schoolmen made up complex, intricate axioms just to protect the
"practice" of the Church.

What causes superstition?
- Pleasure-seeking rites and ceremonies.
- "Performative" holiness (the Pharisee vibe).
- Too much respect for old traditions that just clog up the Church.
- Leaders using religion for their own clout and bags.
- Being too "open" to "good intentions," which just lets in weird delusions and new-age nonsense.
- Trying to understand divine matters using human logic, which just creates a mess of imaginations.
- "Barbarous" times, especially when combined with disasters.

Without a veil, superstition is just deformed. Just like an ape is cringe because it tries too hard to look like
a human, superstition is cringe because it tries to look like religion. And just as good food can rot into worms,
good traditions can rot into a bunch of petty, meaningless rules.

There's also a "meta-superstition" where people try so hard to *avoid* superstition that they just swing to the
opposite extreme. You have to be careful that when you're cleaning out the bad vibes, you don't accidentally throw
away the good ones—which is exactly what happens when the "crowd" is in charge of the reformation.
