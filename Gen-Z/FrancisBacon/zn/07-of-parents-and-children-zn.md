# OF PARENTS AND CHILDREN (FAMILY DRAMA)

Parents' joys are low-key, and so are their fears and sadness; they can't really talk about the wins, and they
won't talk about the losses. Kids make the hustle feel worth it, but they make the failures hit way harder. They
add more stress to life, but they make the thought of death a bit easier. Having kids is just biology; but leaving
behind a legacy of merit and noble works is what makes us human. In fact, some of the most based foundations and
works came from people without kids—they tried to express their mind's image since they couldn't leave a physical
one. The people who care most about the future are often the ones without their own heirs.

The people who start a family dynasty are usually the most indulgent with their kids, seeing them as the continuation
of not just their DNA, but their whole life's work.

The love parents have for their kids is often unequal, and sometimes it's just unfair—especially with moms. As
Solomon said, a wise son makes the dad proud, but a toxic son shames the mother. You'll see houses where the
eldest are respected, the youngest are spoiled, and the ones in the middle are basically forgotten—even though
the forgotten ones often end up being the most based.

When parents are too stingy with their kids' allowance, it's a major fail. it makes the kids low-tier, teaches
them how to hustle and lie, makes them hang out with a bad crowd, and makes them go way too hard when they finally
get money. The best move is for parents to keep their authority but not their wallet—let the kids have the
resources. Also, parents and teachers are kinda mid for creating competition between siblings during childhood,
which often turns into actual beef when they grow up and ruins the family vibe.

Italians don't really differentiate between their own kids and nephews or close relatives—as long as it's the
same bloodline, they're good. Honestly, nature works the same way; sometimes a nephew looks and acts more like
his uncle than his own dad. Parents should help their kids pick their career paths early on, while they're still
flexible. Don't just let them do whatever they "feel" like doing. If a kid has an extraordinary talent, don't
block it; but generally, the move is: "Pick the best path, and habit will make it feel easy." Younger brothers
usually get lucky, but not if the older ones were cheated out of their inheritance.
