# OF CUSTOM AND EDUCATION (THE POWER OF HABIT)

Our thoughts are based on our "inclinations," and our talk is based on our "learning," but our actual *deeds*
are based on our *customs*. As Machiavelli noted, you can't trust "nature" or "big words" unless they're backed
up by habit. For example, if you need someone for a dangerous mission, don't just pick a "fierce" person—pick
someone who has actually "had their hands in blood" before. Nature and promises aren't as strong as custom.

Custom is so powerful that people will swear an oath, make a big promise, and then just go right back to doing
what they've always done—like they're just "engines" moved by the wheels of habit.

The "tyranny of custom" is everywhere. Look at some of the "wise men" in India who sacrifice themselves by fire,
or the wives who want to be burned with their husbands. Or the Spartan boys who were whipped on the altar of Diana
without even flinching. I remember an Irish rebel who asked to be hanged with a "withe" instead of a "halter"
because that's how the rebels before him had been hanged. Or Russian monks who sit in ice water all night. Custom
is the "principal magistrate" of our lives, so we should try to get good customs.

Custom is most powerful when it starts young—that's what we call "education." In youth, the tongue is more
flexible and the body is more supple. "Late learners" struggle to get the hang of things unless they've kept an
open mind and stayed ready for "continual amendment," which is super rare.

When customs are "conjoined" (shared with a group), they're even stronger. Example teaches, company comforts,
competition quickens, and glory raises. If you want to multiply virtues in humans, you need well-organized and
disciplined societies. Good governments nurture virtue once it's grown, but they don't really fix the "seeds." The
tragedy is that we often use these powerful tools for the things we want the least.
