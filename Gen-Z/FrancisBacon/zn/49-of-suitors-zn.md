# OF SUITORS (THE REQUEST GAME)

A lot of "projects" are just mid, and "private suits" (personal requests for favors) often rot the public good. Some
people undertake suits with "bad minds"—not just corrupt, but "crafty." They don't actually intend to perform;
they just want a "thank you," a second reward, or to use the suitor's hope as entertainment. Others use a suit as a
"pretext" to gather info on someone else or to block a competitor.

The Rule of Right:
Every suit should be based on either "equity" (if it's a dispute) or "desert" (if it's a petition). If you're leaning
toward the wrong side in a legal matter, try to "compound" (settle) it rather than just giving a win to the wrong
person. If you're favoring someone less worthy, do it without trash-talking the person who actually deserves the win.

If you don't understand a suit, refer it to a trusted friend before acting. But pick your "referendaries" carefully,
or you'll just be "led by the nose."

The "Pro" Approach to Denying:
People are so tired of being ghosted or abused that "plain dealing" is now seen as gracious. If you're going to
say no, do it early and be honest about the result. Don't pretend to help if you aren't going to.

For "suits of favor":
The first request shouldn't get the most attention. However, if the person provides unique information that you
couldn't get anywhere else, reward them for the "discovery" even if you don't grant the favor.

Tips for the Suitor:
- **Value and Right:** If you don't know the value of what you're asking for, you're being "simple." If you don't
know if you actually have a right to it, you're lacking a conscience.
- **Secrecy:** Keep your suit quiet. If you tell everyone it's "in progress," you might discourage some people,
but you'll definitely wake up the "haters" who will try to block you.
- **Timing:** This is the most important part. Time your request based on the mood of the person granting it and
the moves of those who might cross you.
- **The "Fittest" Mean:** Don't just go to the "biggest" person; go to the most "fit" person. Choose specialists
over generalists.
- **The "Comeback":** If you get a "no," don't act dejected or discontented. If you stay chill, you might get a
"yes" later. But if you don't have a strong "favor" with the person, it's better to just "rise" (withdraw) your
suit before you lose both the favor and the request.

Warning: avoid "general contrivers of suits"—the people who claim they can "get you in" with anyone. They're just
"poison" to the public system.
