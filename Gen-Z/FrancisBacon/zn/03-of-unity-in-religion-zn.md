# OF UNITY IN RELIGION (SQUAD GOALS)

Since religion is the main glue of human society, it's a major win when it's held together by a real sense
of unity. The beef and divisions over religion were things the heathens didn't even know. Why? Because their
religion was more about rituals and aesthetics than any locked-in belief; I mean, their top "church" scholars
were literally poets. But the true God has this trait: he's a jealous God; so his worship and religion don't do
"collabs" or partners. So, let's talk a bit about church unity: the wins, the boundaries, and how to get there.

The wins of unity (besides making God happy, which is the ultimate goal) are two: one for the people outside the
circle, and one for those inside. For the outsiders, heresies and schisms are the biggest red flags—even more
than just being "toxic." Just like a physical wound is worse for the body than a bad mood, a break in unity is
a disaster for the spiritual body. Nothing keeps people out of the church, or drives them away, like a lack of
unity. When one person says, "Look, it's in the desert!" and another says, "No, it's in the inner sanctum!"—when
some look for Christ in the secret meetings of heretics and others in the fake face of a church—people need to
be told constantly: "Don't leave the squad." Paul, who really cared about the outsiders, said: "If an outsider
comes in and hears you speaking in different tongues, won't he think you're just tweaking?" And honestly, he's
not wrong. When atheists hear so many contradicting opinions in religion, it just turns them off and makes them
"sit in the chair of the trolls." It's a light way to put it, but the dysfunction is real. There's even a master
of trolling who listed a fake book called "The Morris-Dance of Heretics," because every sect has its own weird
dance or "cringe" move that just makes non-believers and dirty politicians laugh.

As for those inside, the win is peace, which brings infinite blessings. It locks in the faith and ignites the
love. The outward peace of the church filters down into peace of conscience, and instead of spending all their
time arguing in comment sections, people start writing about self-improvement and devotion.

Regarding the boundaries of unity: where you draw the line is everything. There are two extremes. Some zealots
think any talk of peace is mid. "Is this peace, Jehu?" "What do you care about peace? Get out of my face!" For them,
it's not about peace, it's about the party and the power. On the flip side, some "lukewarm" people think they can
settle religious points with a "middle ground" or some clever compromise, as if they could negotiate a deal between
God and man. Both extremes are a fail. This could be fixed if the Christian league, written by the Savior himself,
were clearly explained in its two main clauses: "He that is not with us is against us," and "He that is not against
us, is with us." Basically, we need to distinguish the "non-negotiables" (the core substance) from things that are
just opinions, traditions, or good intentions. This might seem trivial to some, but if it were done without bias,
everyone would vibe with it.

My small advice on this: people need to stop tearing the church apart over two kinds of beef. First, when the
argument is over something too small and insignificant to be worth the stress—it's just "contradiction for the
sake of contradiction." As one father said, "Christ's coat had no seam, but the church's clothes were of different
colors." There's a difference between unity and uniformity. Second, when the topic is actually big, but it's driven
into such a weird, over-complicated rabbit hole that it becomes more of a "flex" of intelligence than something
substantial. Someone with real judgment can hear ignorant people arguing and know that they actually mean the same
thing, even if they'd never admit it. If this happens between humans, imagine how God, who knows the heart, sees
it. He probably accepts both because he knows they're just struggling with their words. St. Paul warned against
"profane novelties of words" and "false sciences." People create oppositions that aren't even there and put them
into new terms; the term starts to control the meaning instead of the meaning controlling the term.

There are also two "fake" peaces. One is grounded in total ignorance—because every color looks the same in the
dark. The other is based on just admitting that opposites are okay in the core fundamentals—but truth and falsehood
in those things are like iron and clay; they might stick together for a second, but they'll never actually merge.

To get real unity, people have to make sure they don't throw out the laws of love and human society. There are
two swords among Christians: the spiritual and the temporal. Both have their place. But we must never pick up the
"third sword"—the sword of Mahomet—which is trying to grow religion through war or forcing people's consciences
through bloody persecution (unless it's a case of blatant scandal or treason against the state). Even worse is
using religion to fuel riots, authorize conspiracies, or give the sword to the mob to destroy the government,
which is God's order. That's just smashing the first tablet of the law against the second; it's treating people as
"Christians" while forgetting they are "human."

The poet Lucretius, seeing Agamemnon sacrifice his own daughter, cried out: "Look what religion can convince people
to do!" What would he have said if he knew about the massacres in France or the gunpowder plot in England? He would
have been ten times more of an atheist. Just as the temporal sword must be used with extreme caution in religion,
it's absolutely monstrous to give it to the common people. Leave that to the Anabaptists and other furies. It
was a huge flex for the devil to say, "I will ascend and be like the Highest," but it's an even bigger fail to
pretend to be God and say, "I will descend and be like the prince of darkness." What's worse is making the cause
of religion descend to the cruel actions of murdering princes and butchering people. That's not bringing down the
Holy Spirit as a dove, but as a vulture. It's replacing the flag of a Christian church with the flag of pirates
and assassins. It's necessary for the church, the princes, and all the scholars to damn these actions and opinions
and send them to hell. In religious councils, we should remember the apostle's word: "Human anger does not achieve
the righteousness of God." And a wise father once admitted that those who pushed for "forcing the conscience"
were usually just doing it for their own benefit.
