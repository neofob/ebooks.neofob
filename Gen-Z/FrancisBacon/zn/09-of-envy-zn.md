# OF ENVY (THE HATER ENERGY)

Among all the vibes that can basically bewitch or hypnotize someone, only love and envy have that kind of power. Both
are high-key intense; they live in the imagination and manifest in the eyes—especially when the person being
envied is right there, just existing. The Bible calls envy the "evil eye," and astrologers call the bad vibes of
the stars "evil aspects." It's like enthalpy—an irradiation of the eye. Some people have even noticed that envy
hits hardest when the party is peaking in their glory or triumph; that's when the hater energy gets a sharp edge,
and the successful person's aura is most exposed to the blow.

But let's leave the weird stuff and talk about who's actually prone to being a hater, who gets hated on, and the
difference between public and private envy.

A person with zero virtue in themselves will always envy virtue in others. Human minds either feed on their own
wins or on other people's losses. If you can't get the bag or the glow-up yourself, you'll try to drag someone
else down to your level.

People who are way too busy in other people's business are usually haters. Knowing too much about other people's
lives can't be about their own growth; it's just a kind of "play-pleasure" in watching other people's drama. Someone
who just minds their own business doesn't find much to envy; envy is a "gadding passion"—it's always out in the
streets, never staying home.

Old-money nobles are often salty toward "new money" people when they rise, because the social distance changes. It's
like a glitch in the eye: when others move up, they feel like they're moving back.

People who can't fix their own situation—the deformed, the old, the marginalized—often try to ruin others'
situations. Unless, of course, they have a truly heroical nature and decide to make their lack of "standard" parts part
of their flex. They want it to be a miracle that someone "like them" did such great things—like Narses or Tamerlane.

The same goes for people who rise after a total crash. They feel out of sync with the times and think other people's
Ls are a way to redeem their own suffering.

People who try to be the GOAT in too many different things out of vanity are always envious. It's impossible not to
be surpassed by someone in at least one area. Emperor Hadrian was like this—he hated poets and painters because
he wanted to be the best at everything.

Lastly, close relatives and coworkers are the most likely to envy their equals who get promoted. It's a constant
reminder of their own mid-tier status. Cain's envy was especially vile toward Abel because when Abel's sacrifice
was accepted, there wasn't even a crowd watching—it was just pure, concentrated hate.

Now, who gets hated on?
First, people with genuine, eminent virtue are envied less when they rise, because their success feels like a debt
being paid. Nobody hates on someone who actually deserves the win. Also, envy requires a comparison. If there's
no way to compare, there's no envy—which is why kings are only envied by other kings. However, "unworthy" people
are hated most when they first arrive, but they get over it. People of true merit are hated most when their success
lasts a long time, because the new generation grows up and thinks they can do it better.

Old-money people are envied less when they rise because it feels right. Envy is like sunbeams: they hit harder on
a steep cliff than on flat ground. People who rise slowly are envied less than those who "skyrocket" overnight.

People who earned their status through travel, stress, or danger are envied less. People think they paid the
price, and pity always kills envy. That's why high-level politicians often complain about how stressful their
lives are—not because they actually feel it, but to dull the edge of the haters. But this only works if the work
is actually assigned to them; if they're just power-hungry and taking over everything, the hate only grows. The
best way to stop envy is for a leader to let their subordinates keep their own respect and power—that creates
a shield between the leader and the haters.

Above all, the people who get hated the most are those who flex their success in an arrogant, proud way—people
who only feel good when they're showing off their pomp or winning over competition. Wise people "sacrifice" to
envy by letting themselves be crossed or defeated in things that don't really matter. Honestly, being great in a
plain and open way (without the ego) draws less envy than being crafty and cunning.

To wrap this up: since envy is basically witchcraft, the only cure is the "witchcraft cure"—move the target. The
smart leaders always have a "fall guy" or a "shield" to take the heat for them—sometimes a servant, sometimes a
colleague. There are always some power-hungry people who are happy to take the heat as long as they get some authority.

Public envy is different. There's actually some good in it, whereas private envy is pure toxicity. Public envy is
like "ostracism"—it cancels people when they get too big, keeping them in check.

In modern terms, this public envy is "discontentment." It's like an infection in a state. Once it starts, it ruins
even the best actions, turning them into something "stinky." Trying to fix it with a few "nice" actions doesn't
work; it just looks like you're scared, which makes the infection spread faster.

This public hate usually hits the ministers more than the kings. But if the ministers are getting hated on for no
real reason, or if everyone is getting hated on, the hate is actually directed at the state itself.

Finally, envy is the most persistent of all emotions. Other vibes come and go, but envy never takes a holiday. It's
the vilest emotion, which is why it's the trademark of the devil—the "Envious Man" who sows weeds among the
wheat at night. Envy always works in the dark, subtilely, to ruin good things.
