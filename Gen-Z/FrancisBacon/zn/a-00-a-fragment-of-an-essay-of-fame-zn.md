# A FRAGMENT OF AN ESSAY OF FAME (THE HYPE CYCLE)

The poets describe Fame as a monster: a thousand eyes, a thousand tongues, a thousand voices. It's a bit "extra,"
but there are some real points. Fame "gathers strength as it goes"; it's a "terror to great cities"; and it often
mixes "what happened" with "what didn't happen."

One of the most interesting takes: the Earth brought forth Fame as a daughter of the giants. This means that
"rebels" (the giants) and "seditious rumors" (Fame) are basically siblings. If you can "tame" this monster and
use it to hunt other "birds of prey," then it's actually useful.

In politics, "fame" is a huge tool.
- **Mucianus** ruined Vitellius by spreading a rumor that he was going to swap the legions of Syria and Germany.
- **Julius Caesar** caught Pompey off guard by spreading a rumor that his own soldiers hated him.
- **Livia** secured her son Tiberius's succession by constantly leaking that her husband, Augustus, was recovering.
- **The Grand Turk**'s death was hidden from the Janissaries to prevent them from sacking the city.
- **Themistocles** tricked Xerxes into leaving Greece by pretending the Grecians were going to destroy his bridge
of ships.

There are thousands of examples. Wise governors should watch "fame" as closely as they watch "actions."
