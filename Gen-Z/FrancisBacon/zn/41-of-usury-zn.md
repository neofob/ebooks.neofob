# OF USURY (THE INTEREST GAME)

A lot of people have roasted usury with witty takes. They say it's a shame the devil gets God's share (the tithe);
that the usurer is the ultimate Sabbath-breaker because his "plough" (the interest) keeps working every single Sunday;
and that the usurer is a "drone"—just living off others' work. They say usury breaks the first law for humans:
"by the sweat of your brow shall you eat your bread," not "by the sweat of *someone else's* brow." Some even say
usurers should wear orange-tawny hats to mark them as "Judaizers," and that it's just against nature for money to
create more money.

Personally, I say usury is a "necessary evil" because people are hard-hearted and won't just lend money for
free. Some have tried to come up with "cunning" alternatives like banks or "estate discoveries," but few have
actually talked about usury in a useful way. Let's weigh the Ls and the Ws.

The Ls (Disadvantages):
1. **Fewer Merchants:** If people can just make easy money from usury, they won't take the risk of starting a
business. Merchandising is the "main vein" of wealth in a state; usury is a clog in that vein.
2. **Poor Merchants:** High interest rates are like high rent for a farmer—it makes it impossible to actually
grow the business.
3. **State Decay:** When trade drops, the state's tax revenue (customs) drops too.
4. **Wealth Concentration:** Usurers play it safe with "certainties," while merchants take risks. In the end,
the usurer usually ends up with all the money in the box. A state thrives when wealth is spread out, not hoarded.
5. **Land Value Drop:** Since money is tied up in usury, fewer people are buying or improving land.
6. **Innovation Kill:** Usury is a "slug" that dampens industry and new inventions.
7. **Estate Ruin:** It's the cancer that destroys individual estates, leading to general public poverty.

The Ws (Advantages):
1. **Trade Booster:** Actually, it helps some trade. Most young merchants start their hustle by borrowing at
interest. If usurers stopped lending, trade would just freeze.
2. **Safety Net:** Easy borrowing prevents people from having to sell their lands or goods at a massive loss during
a crisis. Usury "gnaws" at you, but a bad market "swallows" you whole.
3. **Reality Check:** It's a fantasy to think people would lend money for zero profit. Trying to ban usury is a
"Utopian" dream that doesn't work in the real world.

The Fix:
We need to "grind the tooth" of usury so it doesn't bite too hard, but still keep the money flowing to the
merchants. The move is to have two different rates:
1. **General Rate:** A low, free rate (like 5% per year) for everyone. This helps common borrowers and encourages
people to invest in land or improvements instead of just hoarding cash.
2. **Merchant Rate:** A higher rate, but only for licensed lenders and known merchants in specific trade hubs. This
ensures that the most lucrative businesses can still get the funding they need.

The state should take a small fee for the license, but otherwise let the lender and merchant handle it. This
prevents the "safe" money in the country from being sucked away by a few high-interest lenders in the cities.

Some might say this "authorizes" usury, but it's better to regulate it openly than to let it "rage" in the shadows.
