# OF A KING (THE ULTIMATE BOSS)

A king is basically a "mortal God" on earth. God lent him His name as a huge honor, but reminded him that he still
has to die like any other human, so he doesn't get too "full of himself."

God is the least "beholden" to humans—He does everything for them, and they do almost nothing for Him.

A king who doesn't feel his crown is "too heavy" must wear it every day. But if he thinks it's "too light," he
doesn't understand what the crown is actually made of (responsibility).

The Golden Rules:
1. **Religion is the Law:** Use religion as the rule for government, not as a "weight" to balance the scales. If you
only use religion when it's convenient, you're "too light" in the eyes of God, and your kingdom will be taken from you.
2. **The Counsel Gap:** A king should be able to give advice himself, but he shouldn't "rely" on his own ideas. It's
better for a "bad outcome" to be blamed on a subject's advice than on the king's own judgment.
3. **The Fountain of Honor:** A king is the source of honor. He shouldn't let "courtiers" sell that honor for a
profit, or the "water" loses its virtue.
4. **The Life of the Law:** The king animates the "dead letter" of the law, making it active through rewards
and punishments.
5. **Avoid "Rapid" Law Changes:** New government is always dangerous. Changing fundamental laws is a "nuclear
option" that suggests the only way to get the crown was through conquest.
6. **Don't Sell Justice:** A king who sells "seats of justice" is just teaching his judges to be corrupt.
7. **Bounty vs. Prodigality:** Being generous is a royal virtue. But a "prodigal" (wasteful) king is basically a
tyrant, because he'll eventually have to "take" from the people to fill his holes.
8. **The Balance of Love and Fear:** A king who isn't feared isn't loved. Study to be feared *because* you are
loved, not the other way around.
9. **Mercy and Justice:** Resemble God—be merciful, but don't let a "man of death" live. If you're too merciful
to the wicked, you lose the love of the good.
10. **Beware the Hype-Men:** A king's greatest enemies are his flatterers. They always agree with him, but their
words actually work against him.
11. **Public vs. Private Love:** A king's love should be for the "public good," not for one specific person. Still,
it's necessary to have a few "worthy" favorites.

The "Red Flags" for a King:
- **Simulated Sanctity:** Fake holiness in the church (two times the iniquity).
- **Useless Equity:** "Mid" mercy in the chancery (clumsy mercy).
- **Useful Iniquity:** Using "corruption" in the exchequer (cruel robbery).
- **Faithful Rashness:** A general who is "loyal" but reckless (leads to late regrets).
- **Infidel Prudence:** A secretary who is "smart" but lacks faith (a snake in the grass).

Conclusion: The king has the most power, but he also has the most stress. He's the "servant of his people." If
you don't respect the king, you're basically an atheist, because you lack the "fear of God" in your heart.
