# OF YOUTH AND AGE (THE AGE GAP)

A person can be young in years but "old in hours" if they didn't waste their time, but that's a rare flex. Generally,
youth is like a "first draft"—not as wise as the second. Young people have a more lively "invention," and ideas
stream into their minds more divinely. However, people with "high heat" (intense desires and emotions) aren't ready
for the big leagues until they're past their prime. Julius Caesar and Septimus Severus were like this—they were
chaotic in their youth but became the ablest leaders later. Then you have "chill" natures (like Augustus Caesar)
who can kill it even while they're young.

Conversely, having "youthful energy" in old age is a top-tier composition for business.

The breakdown:
- **Youth:** Better at inventing than judging, better at execution than strategy, and better at "new projects"
than "settled business." Their mistakes are often total ruins.
- **Age:** Experience guides them in familiar things, but can actually lead them astray in new situations. Their
mistakes are usually just "could've done more" or "could've done it sooner."

Young people tend to:
- Embrace more than they can hold.
- Fly to the end without thinking about the steps.
- Follow random principles they happened to find.
- Innovate without considering the drawbacks.
- Use "nuclear options" immediately.
- Refuse to admit they're wrong (like a horse that won't stop or turn).

Older people tend to:
- Object too much.
- Over-consult and hesitate.
- Adventure too little.
- Repent too soon.
- Settle for "mid" success instead of finishing the job.

The best move is to pair them up. The virtues of one age fix the defects of the other. Young people can learn
from the actors, and old people can be energized by the learners. Plus, it covers all bases: old people bring the
authority, and young people bring the popularity.

A rabbin once said that young people "see visions" and old people "dream dreams," suggesting that youth is closer to
a clear revelation from God. As you "drink" more of the world, it intoxicates you. Age improves your understanding,
but it doesn't necessarily improve your will or your emotions.

Some people "ripen" too early, but their glow fades fast. This happens if they have "brittle wits" (they get stupid
later in life), if their "grace" was only tied to their youth (like a fluent way of speaking that sounds cringe
in an old man), or if they set their ambitions too high too early for their life span to sustain.
