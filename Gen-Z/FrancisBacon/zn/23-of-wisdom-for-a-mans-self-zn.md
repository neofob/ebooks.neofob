# OF WISDOM FOR A MAN’S SELF (THE MAIN CHARACTER SYNDROME)

An ant is "wise" for itself, but it's a pest in a garden. Similarly, people who are obsessed with their own success
often end up wasting the public's resources. You have to find a balance between self-love and society. Be true to
yourself, but don't be a traitor to others—especially to your boss or your country.

Making everything about yourself is a "low-tier" way to live. You're basically just a piece of earth standing still
on its own center, while everything else in the universe moves to benefit something else. This is more "tolerable"
for a king, because the king *is* the state—his wins and losses are the public's wins and losses. But for a
servant or a citizen, it's a disaster. If you crook everything to your own ends, you'll inevitably clash with the
goals of your master or the state.

The worst part is the total lack of proportion. It's bad enough to put your own needs before your boss's, but
it's "extreme" when a tiny win for the servant causes a massive loss for the boss. This is exactly how corrupt
officers, ambassadors, and generals work—they set a "bias" on the game for their own petty ends, ruining huge,
important affairs. These "self-lovers" would literally set their own house on fire just to roast some eggs. They
often keep a good reputation with their bosses because they spend all their time "pleasing" them while actually
just profiting themselves.

"Wisdom for oneself" is often just a depraved habit. It's the "wisdom" of rats (leaving the house just before
it collapses), the "wisdom" of foxes (kicking out the badger who did the hard work of digging the hole), or the
"wisdom" of crocodiles (crying while they eat you). People who are their own only rivals often end up unlucky;
they sacrifice their whole lives to themselves, only to become sacrifices to the randomness of fortune. They
thought they could "pinion the wings" of fate with their self-wisdom, but they were wrong.
