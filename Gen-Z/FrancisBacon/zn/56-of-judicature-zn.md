# OF JUDICATURE (THE LEGAL GRIND)

Judges need to remember their job is to "interpret the law," not "make the law." If they just make things up,
they're like the Church of Rome—using "exposition" as a cover to add and alter rules and introduce "novelty"
under the guise of antiquity. A judge should be more learned than witty, more respected than "plausible," and more
cautious than confident.

Integrity is the a must. A judge who moves a "landmark" (changes a boundary) is an unjust judge who ruins
everything. One bad sentence does more damage than many bad examples—it "corrupts the fountain," not just the stream.

Dealing with the parties:
- **The "Trolls":** Some people turn judgment into "wormwood" or "vinegar" through injustice and delays.
- **The "Bully" and the "Snake":** A judge's job is to suppress "force" (open bullying) and "fraud" (hidden tricks).
- **The "Noise":** Contentious suits should be "spewed out" like a bad meal.
- **The "Leveler":** A judge should balance the scale. If one side has more power, money, or "cunning," the judge
must "raise the valleys and take down the hills" to make the ground even for a fair judgment.
- **The "Lax" vs. "Rigid":** Don't use "hard constructions" or "strained inferences." Penal laws shouldn't be used
as a "shower of snares" to trap people. If a law is too old or unfit for the times, a wise judge should "confine"
its execution. In life-and-death cases, remember mercy for the person even if you're severe about the example.

Dealing with the lawyers:
- **Listen up:** A judge who interrupts or over-speaks is like a "badly tuned cymbal."
- **Don't "speed-run" evidence:** Don't cut off counsel just to show how "quick" your mind is.
- **The 4-Step Process:** 1. Direct the evidence. 2. Moderate the length/repetition. 3. Summarize the material
points. 4. Give the sentence. Anything more is just "flexing" or impatience.
- **Don't be a "fan" of bold lawyers:** Just because a lawyer is aggressive doesn't mean they're right. Also,
avoid "favorite" lawyers—it creates "by-ways" (bribes) and suspicion.
- **The Give-and-Take:** Praise a lawyer who handles a case well, even if their client loses—it keeps the client's
respect for their lawyer. But "call out" lawyers who use cunning, neglect their cases, or are too bold.

Dealing with the clerks:
The court is a "hallowed place." If the clerks are corrupt, the whole process is ruined. You can't get "sweet fruit"
from a "thorny bush." Watch out for:
- **Suit-Sowers:** People who create fake cases just to make the court look busy and the country suffer.
- **Court-Parasites:** People who push the court beyond its bounds for their own "scraps."
- **The "Left Hands":** People who use "sinister tricks" to turn a straight case into a labyrinth.
- **Fee-Collectors:** People who treat the court like a "bush" where the sheep come for protection but lose their
"fleece" (money).
A seasoned, wary clerk who knows the precedents is a huge asset—they're like a "finger" that points the judge
in the right direction.

Relationship with the State:
The ultimate rule is "Salus populi suprema lex"—the safety of the people is the highest law. Laws are useless
if they don't serve this end. Kings and judges should consult each other often.

Judges should be "lions under the throne"—strong and brave, but respectful of sovereignty. And they should
remember that their real power is a "wise application" of the laws, not just robotic adherence to them.
