# OF DISCOURSE (THE ART OF THE CHAT)

Some people just want to be seen as "witty" because they can talk about anything, but they have zero "judgment"
when it comes to what's actually true. It's a bit mid to know *what* could be said but not know what *should* be
thought. Some people just have a few "go-to" topics and no variety, which is tedious and honestly kinda ridiculous
once you catch on.

The real pro move in a conversation is knowing how to start a topic and when to pivot to something else—that's
how you "lead the dance." The best chats are a mix: current events blended with arguments, stories mixed with logic,
questions mixed with opinions, and jokes mixed with serious talk. If you just stay on one note, you're just "jading"
the conversation.

As for jokes, some things are just off-limits: religion, state business, high-status people, anyone's current
emergency, or anything that actually deserves pity. Some people think they're being "edgy" by darting out piquant
comments, but that energy needs to be bridled. You have to know the difference between "salty" (witty) and "bitter"
(toxic). If you're the type to roast everyone, just remember that people have memories.

If you ask a lot of questions, you'll learn a lot and make people feel good, especially if you ask things that let
them flex their own skills. Just don't be annoying—don't be a "poser" who asks just to ask. And let other people
speak. If someone is trying to main-character the whole conversation, find a way to pivot them off and bring others in.

Also, a pro tip: if you act like you don't know something you actually do, people will eventually think you know
things you *don't*. And don't talk about yourself too much; it's usually a red flag. The only time it's a flex
to talk about yourself is when you're praising a virtue in someone else that you also happen to have. And avoid
shading others—discourse should be like a field, not a targeted attack.

Discretion is more important than eloquence. Speaking in a way that vibes with the person you're talking to is
better than using "perfect" words or a "perfect" order. A long speech without a good back-and-forth just shows
you're slow. A quick reply without a solid foundation shows you're shallow. It's like the difference between a
greyhound and a hare: the weakest in the straight are often the nimblest in the turn. Finally, don't take too long
to get to the point—that's exhausting—but don't be so blunt that you're rude.
