# OF RICHES (THE MONEY MINDSET)

I don't think riches are "better" than virtue; they're more like the "baggage" of virtue. Like baggage for an army,
you can't really leave it behind, but it slows down your march and can even mess up the victory. Great riches
don't actually have a "use" other than giving them away. The rest is just for the "flex." Solomon said it best:
when someone has a lot, there are just more people around to consume it. The owner just gets to "look" at it.

People set fake, sky-high prices on "little stones" (diamonds) and build massive monuments just to pretend that
great riches have a purpose. Some say money is a "stronghold" that can buy you out of trouble, but that's mostly
in the imagination. In reality, great riches have often "sold" more people than they've bought out (through greed
and corruption).

The goal should be: don't chase "proud" riches. Get what you can justly, use it soberly, give it away cheerfully,
and leave the rest contentedly. But don't be "holier-than-thou" about money. Use it as a tool for good. Also, don't
rush the bag. Riches that come too fast are usually "sus." The poets say that when Plutus (Riches) is sent from
Jupiter, he limps—meaning just labor and honest work take time. But when he's sent from Pluto (or the devil),
he runs—meaning fraud and oppression bring the money fast.

There are many ways to get rich, and most of them are foul. Being "stingy" is one way, but it's not innocent because
it stops you from being charitable. The most "natural" way is improving the land—it's the earth's blessing. It's
slow, but if you're already wealthy and you invest in farming (grazing, timber, corn, iron, etc.), your wealth
will multiply exponentially.

Once you have a huge "stock," you can expect the "prime of the markets"—you can take the big deals that smaller
players can't afford. Ordinary trades are honest and grow through diligence and a good rep. But "deal-making"
(arbitrage) is shadier; it involves waiting for others to be desperate or using "agents" to trick people. "Chopping"
bargains (buying just to flip them) basically scams both the seller and the buyer.

Sharing profits with others can make you rich if you pick the right partners. Usury (charging high interest)
is the most "certain" way to make money, but it's one of the worst because you're basically eating the bread of
someone else's sweat. Also, it's risky because brokers might set you up with "unsound" borrowers.

Being the first to invent something or getting a special privilege can lead to an "overgrowth" in riches. If you
have both the "invention" and the "judgment" to execute it at the right time, you can do big things. Don't put
everything on "adventures" (high-risk bets), or you'll go broke. Balance your risks with some certainties. Monopolies
are another way to get rich, especially if you know what's going to be in demand before everyone else does.

Money earned through "service" to others is a good start, but if it's earned through flattery and "feeding humors,"
it's low-tier. Even worse is "fishing for wills" (trying to get into a dying person's testament).

Don't trust people who "despise" riches; they usually just despair of getting them, and they're often the worst
when they finally do get the bag. Also, don't be "penny-wise." Money has wings—sometimes it flies away on its own,
but sometimes you have to "set it flying" (invest it) to bring more back.

When leaving an inheritance, moderate portions work best. A massive estate left to a young heir is just a "lure"
for all the "birds of prey" to rip him apart. Similarly, huge "charitable foundations" can become just "painted
tombs" that rot from the inside. Measure your success by "measure," not just "quantity," and don't wait until
you're dead to be charitable. If you only give after you're gone, you're just being liberal with someone else's money.
