# OF PLANTATIONS (THE COLONY STARTER PACK)

Establishing a new settlement is an "ancient and heroical" move. Back in the day, the world was just producing
"children" (new colonies) left and right, but now it's an old world. I'm all for "plantations" in pure soil—meaning
you're not just kicking people out of their homes to make room for yours; otherwise, it's just an "extirpation,"
not a plantation.

Starting a colony is like planting a forest: you have to accept that you won't see a profit for maybe twenty
years. The biggest reason most colonies crash is because people try to "speed-run" the profit in the first few
years. Sure, quick wins are nice, but not if they ruin the long-term vibe.

Also, don't ever use the "scum of the earth" or convicted criminals to start a colony. Not only is that a moral
fail, but it's a strategic one. They'll just live like rogues, be lazy, cause drama, waste the food, and then
dip back to their home country, giving the whole project a bad rep. You need a "squad" of actual pros: gardeners,
farmers, laborers, smiths, carpenters, joiners, fishermen, and a few pharmacists, surgeons, cooks, and bakers.

When you arrive, first check what the land already gives you for free—chestnuts, walnuts, olives, dates, cherries,
wild honey, etc. Then, plant things that grow fast (under a year), like carrots, turnips, onions, and maize. Don't
stress about wheat or barley right away—they're too much work. Start with peas and beans; they're low-maintenance
and can be used for both meat and bread. Bring plenty of biscuits and flour until you can grow your own. For animals,
pick the ones that don't get sick easily and multiply fast: pigs, goats, chickens, turkeys, and geese.

Manage your food like you're in a besieged town—strict allowances. Keep a "common stock" of gardens and corn,
and distribute it proportionally, though you can let people have a few spots for their own private use. Figure
out what the land produces that you can sell to offset the costs—maybe timber, iron, or silk—but don't get
obsessed with mining. The hope of finding gold often makes planters lazy with everything else.

For the government: one leader, a few advisors, and the power to use "martial law" (within limits). Most importantly,
use the wilderness as a way to keep God and his service front and center. Don't let the government be run by too
many "investors" back home—they only care about the immediate bag. Use noblemen and gentlemen instead. Give the
settlers a break from taxes until the colony is strong, and let them sell their goods wherever they get the best price.

Don't overpopulate too quickly. Send supplies based on how people are actually surviving, not just a random
number. Also, don't build everything right along the coast or rivers in swampy, "unwholesome" ground—move the
buildings "upwards" to avoid the illness. Make sure you have plenty of salt for the food.

If you're dealing with indigenous people, don't just give them "trinkets and gingles." Treat them justly and
graciously, keep a guard, and don't help them attack their enemies (though defending them is fine). Invite some
of them to visit the home country so they can see the "glow-up" and come back telling everyone how great it is.

Once the colony is stable, start bringing in women. You need generations, not just a rotating cast of men. And
finally, the most "sinful" thing you can do is abandon a colony that's already starting to work. It's a dishonor
and it's basically murder.
