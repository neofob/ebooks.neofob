# OF REGIMEN OF HEALTH (THE BIOHACKING GUIDE)

There's a kind of wisdom here that goes beyond medical textbooks. The best "physic" is your own observation:
knowing what makes you feel good and what makes you feel like trash. It's safer to say, "This doesn't vibe with me,
so I'm stopping," than to say, "I feel fine, so I'll keep doing it." In your youth, your body can handle a lot of
"excess," but that's just a loan you'll have to pay back as you get older. Age can't be defied, so don't expect
to do the same things forever.

Avoid sudden, massive changes in your diet. If you have to change something, adjust everything else to fit. It's
a secret of both nature and state: it's safer to change many small things than one big thing. Examine your
habits—diet, sleep, exercise, clothes—and if you find something hurtful, phase it out slowly. If the change
feels off, go back and try again. It's hard to tell the difference between what's globally "healthy" and what's
specifically fit for *your* body.

One of the best "life hacks" for longevity is to be free-minded and cheerful during meals, sleep, and exercise. As
for your mental state: avoid envy, anxious fears, "inward" anger, and over-thinking "knotty" problems. Also,
avoid extreme highs (excessive joy) and unshared sadness. Instead, focus on hopes, mirth, variety, and a sense of
wonder. Fill your mind with "splendid" things—histories, fables, and the wonders of nature.

Don't make medicine your whole personality. If you avoid it completely while you're healthy, your body will be
shocked when you actually need it. But if you use it too much, it loses its effect. I recommend specific diets
for certain seasons rather than constant medication.

Don't ignore any "new accident" in your body—ask a pro about it. In sickness, focus on getting back to health;
in health, focus on action. If you've pushed your body to be strong while healthy, you can often recover from most
illnesses just with diet and "tendering."

Celsus had a great tip: vary and interchange opposites, but always lean toward the "benign" (gentle) extreme. Try
fasting and full eating (but lean toward full); stay up and sleep (but lean toward sleep); sit and exercise (but
lean toward exercise). This keeps nature happy but also trains it.

When picking a doctor, be careful. Some are too "nice" and just agree with the patient instead of curing the
disease. Others are too "by the book" and ignore the actual condition of the patient. Find someone in the middle;
or if you can't, hire two different types. And always involve the person who knows your body best, along with the
person who has the best reputation.
