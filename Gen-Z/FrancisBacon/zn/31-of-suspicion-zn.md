# OF SUSPICION (THE PARANOIA VIBE)

Suspicion is like a bat among birds—it only flies at twilight. You've gotta shut it down or at least keep it
on a leash, because it clouds your headspace, kills your friendships, and glitches your workflow, so nothing gets
done. It turns kings into tyrants, husbands into jealous wrecks, and wise people into indecisive, melancholy messes.

It's not a "heart" issue; it's a "brain" issue. Even the strongest people can get paranoid—like Henry VII of
England. But when a strong person is suspicious, they actually check if the vibe is real before they react, so it
doesn't do much damage. In fearful people, though, suspicion spreads like a virus.

The biggest trigger for paranoia? Not knowing enough. If you're in the dark, you start imagining things. The fix is
simple: get more tea. Don't just let your suspicions simmer in the dark. And let's be real—do you actually think
the people you work with are saints? They're going to chase their own bag and be loyal to themselves first. The
best way to handle suspicion is to assume it *might* be true, but manage it as if it's false. Basically, prepare
for the worst so it can't hurt you, but don't let it ruin the vibe.

The suspicions your mind just makes up are just "buzzes," but the ones people plant in your head through gossip and
whispering—those have stings. The best way to clear the air is to just be 100 with the person you suspect. You'll
either find out the truth, or you'll make them realize you're onto them, so they stop doing whatever was suspicious. But
don't try this with low-tier people; once they know they're suspected, they'll never be real with you again. As
the Italians say, suspicion is like a passport for faith—it should push you to get the truth out in the open.
