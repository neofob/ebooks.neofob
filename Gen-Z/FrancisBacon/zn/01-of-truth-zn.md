# OF TRUTH (THE REAL TEA)

"What's the real tea?" Pilate asked, trolling for a bit, then dipped before the answer even dropped. Some people
just love the chaos, fr—they think locking in a belief is a total mid-tier prison, chasing that "free spirit"
energy in their thoughts and their moves. Even though the old-school philosophers who lived for the plot are gone,
there are still these "intellectuals" today doing the same thing, though they've got way less rizz than the ancients.

It's not just that finding the truth is a whole struggle, or that once you find it, it forces you to change your
vibe; it's that people actually *lowkey love* the lie. Some Greek scholars tried to figure out why people vibe with
lies when it's not even for the plot (like poets) or for the bag (like merchants), but just for the lie itself. I
don't even know, man. The truth is like high-definition daylight—it doesn't let the masks and the fake-flexing
of the world look half as aesthetic as they do under dim candlelight.

Truth is maybe like a pearl—it glows best in the sunlight—but it doesn't have the same price tag as a diamond
or a ruby that sparkles in the weird lighting. A bit of a lie always adds some spice. Like, if you stripped away all
the delusional takes, the fake hopes, and the over-hyped valuations from people's heads, most of them would be left
feeling completely empty, just shrunken things full of sadness and zero motivation. One of the early church fathers
called poetry "the wine of demons" because it fills the head with a "shadow" of a lie. But it's not the lie that
just passes through the mind that's the problem—it's the lie that sinks in and settles, that's where the real L is.

But regardless of how messed up human judgment is, truth (which is the only thing that can truly judge itself)
teaches us that the hunt for truth is like the courtship, the knowledge of truth is its presence, and the belief
in truth is just enjoying the vibe. That's the ultimate win for human nature. God started by breathing light into
the world; he ended by breathing light into the mind; and since then, he's been downloading enlightenment into
the spirits of the chosen.

A poet once said it perfectly: "It's a vibe to stand on the shore and watch ships getting tossed by the sea;
it's a mood to stand in a castle window and watch a battle go down below; but nothing hits like standing on
the vantage-ground of truth" (a peak that no one can conquer, where the air is always clear and the vibes are
immaculate), "and watching the errors, the wandering, the mists, and the storms in the valley below"—as long as
you're watching with pity, not flexxing your superiority. Truly, it's heaven on earth when a person's mind moves
in love, rests in providence, and spins on the axis of truth.

Moving from the deep philosophy to the daily hustle: anyone can see that being straight-up and transparent is the
highest honor for a human. Mixing in lies is like putting cheap alloy in gold or silver—it might make the metal
easier to work with, but it ruins the purity. These twisting, crooked paths are the way of the snake—crawling
low on the belly instead of walking tall. There's no bigger L, no more shame, than being caught as a fake or a
traitor. Montaigne put it beautifully: saying someone lies is basically saying they're "brave" towards God but a
coward towards people. Because a lie faces God and shrinks from man. Seriously, the toxicity of lying and breaking
faith is the final warning before the end of the world; it's written that when the Messiah returns, he won't find
a shred of real faith left on earth.
