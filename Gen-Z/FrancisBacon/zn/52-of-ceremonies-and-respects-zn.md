# OF CEREMONIES AND RESPECTS (THE ETIQUETTE FLEX)

If you're "just real" and have zero filter, you need to have an insane amount of virtue to pull it off—like a diamond
set without any foil. But for most of us, "good forms" (manners) are like perpetual letters of recommendation. Small,
consistent gestures of respect often win more points than one giant act of virtue, because people notice the small
things every day.

To get this right, just don't despise ceremonies. If you observe them in others, you'll naturally pick them up. But
don't try too hard—if you're too "measured" in your behavior, you look like a robot, and it's hard to imagine
you can handle big, complex matters if you're obsessed with tiny details.

Not using ceremonies at all is a mistake—it tells others they don't need to respect you. But "over-indexing" on
them is also a fail; it's tedious and makes you look untrustworthy. The pro move is to hide "real" and impactful
messages inside polite compliments.

The "vibe" should change based on who you're with:
- **With Peers:** Keep it low-key. You're already familiar, so there's no need to "keep state."
- **With Inferiors:** Be a bit more familiar. You already have their reverence, so being approachable is a win.

If you're too much of anything, you become "cheap." When you agree with someone, don't just be a "yes-man." Add a
little something of your own—a distinction, a condition, or an extra reason. This shows you're actually thinking
and not just following.

Also, don't be "too perfect" with your compliments. Your haters will just use your politeness to distract from your
actual virtues. In business, being "too respectful" or too careful about timing can be a loss. As Solomon said,
"He that considereth the wind shall not sow." A wise person creates their own opportunities instead of waiting
for the perfect moment.

Your behavior should be like your clothes: not too tight or "over-designed," but free enough for you to actually
move and act.
