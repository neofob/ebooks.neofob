# OF NEGOTIATING (THE HUSTLER'S HANDBOOK)

Generally, talking in person is better than sending a letter, and using a "third party" is better than doing
it yourself.

When to use letters:
- When you want to force a written answer.
- When you need a "paper trail" for justification later.
- When you're afraid of being interrupted or overheard.

When to deal in person:
- When your "face" (presence) creates respect (especially with subordinates).
- In "tender" cases where you need to read their facial expressions to know how far to push.
- When you want the freedom to either "deny" or "explain" your position on the fly.

Picking your "Instruments" (Agents):
Don't just pick the "smartest" person. Pick the one who fits the job:
- **Plain types:** Best for just getting the job done and reporting back honestly.
- **Passionate types:** Best for things they actually care about.
- **Bold types:** Best for "expostulation" (arguing/demanding).
- **Fair-spoken types:** Best for persuasion.
- **Crafty types:** Best for digging for info.
- **"Absurd" types:** Best for business that doesn't make much sense on its own.
- **Lucky types:** People who have won before bring confidence.

The Pro Moves:
1. **Sound them out:** Don't just drop the main point immediately. Test the waters first, unless you're trying to
surprise them with a "short question."
2. **Target the "Hungry":** It's easier to deal with people who are still "in appetite" (want something) than
people who already have everything they want.
3. **The First Move:** In a deal with conditions, the "first performance" is everything. You can only demand this
if the nature of the thing requires it, or if you can convince them you'll need them for something else later,
or if you're seen as the "honester" man.
4. **Discovery vs. Working:** People reveal themselves when they trust you, when they're emotional, when they're
caught off guard, or when they're desperate for something and can't find a "pretext."
5. **Handling People:** To "work" someone, you need to know their:
   - Nature/Fashions $\rightarrow$ Lead them.
   - Ends $\rightarrow$ Persuade them.
   - Weaknesses $\rightarrow$ Awe them.
   - Interests $\rightarrow$ Govern them.
6. **Dealing with Cunning People:** Focus on their *ends* to interpret what they're saying. Keep your talk short
and say things they don't expect.
7. **The Slow Burn:** In hard negotiations, don't expect to "sow and reap" at once. Prepare the business and let it
"ripen" by degrees.
