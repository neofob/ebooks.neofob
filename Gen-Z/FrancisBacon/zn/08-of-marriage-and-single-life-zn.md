# OF MARRIAGE AND SINGLE LIFE (THE RELATIONSHIP DEBATE)

Someone who has a wife and kids has basically given hostages to fortune. They're just anchors that stop you from
doing big things, whether those things are virtuous or just chaotic. Honestly, the most impactful works for the
public usually come from unmarried or childless people—they "married" the public instead and gave all their
energy and resources to the world. Still, it makes sense that parents care more about the future, since they have
to pass their legacy to their kids.

Some single people just live for themselves and think the future is irrelevant. Others view a wife and kids as just
a bunch of bills. Then you have the rich, greedy types who flex about having no children just so they can seem
wealthier—as if having kids is a tax on their net worth. But the most common reason for staying single is just
wanting that freedom, especially for those "main character" personalities who feel like even their belt is a shackle.

Single men make the best friends, the best bosses, and the best employees; but they're not always the best
citizens, because they're the first ones to dip when things get rough. A single life works well for church leaders,
because it's hard to pour love into the community if you have to fill your own family's pool first. For judges,
it doesn't really matter; if they're corrupt, a bad employee is five times worse than a bad wife. For soldiers,
generals usually remind them of their families to motivate them; in fact, the way some cultures despise marriage
just makes the average soldier more low-tier.

Wife and kids are basically a "humanity bootcamp." Single men might be more charitable because they have more cash,
but they can also be more cruel and hard-hearted (which is why they make great inquisitors)—their "soft side"
just doesn't get used. Steady, traditional types usually make the most loving husbands—like Ulysses, who chose
his old wife over immortality.

Chaste women can be proud and difficult, flexing their virtue. A wife is more likely to be loyal and obedient
if she thinks her husband is actually wise—which she'll never believe if he's just jealous. Wives are like
mistresses for young men, companions for middle age, and nurses for old men. You can find a reason to marry at
any age, but one wise man's take was: "A young man? Not yet. An older man? Not at all."

You often see bad husbands with amazing wives. Maybe it makes the husband's rare moments of kindness feel more
valuable, or maybe the wives take pride in their patience. But this always happens when the wives chose the bad
husbands themselves against their friends' advice—then they're just committed to making their own mistake work.
