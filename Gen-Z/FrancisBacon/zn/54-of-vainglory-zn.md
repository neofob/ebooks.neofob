# OF VAINGLORY (THE CLOUT CHASE)

There's a story about a fly sitting on a chariot wheel saying, "Look at all the dust *I'm* raising!" That's a
perfect metaphor for vain people. They think they're the ones driving the success, even if they had zero hand in it.

People who are obsessed with their own image (the "glorious") usually end up being "factious"—they need constant
comparisons to feel superior. They're also "violent" because they have to prove their own hype. They can't be secret,
which means they're rarely "effectual." It's the classic "much bruit, little fruit" (lots of noise, zero results).

Still, vainglory has its uses in the real world. If you need to create a "vibe" or a reputation for virtue or
greatness, these "clout-chasers" are the perfect trumpeters. Also, some "cross lies" (strategic misinformation)
can actually produce results. If a negotiator tells two princes that the other is way stronger than they actually
are, it might trick them into joining a war. Lies can create an "opinion," and opinion can eventually become a reality.

In the military, a bit of vainglory is essential. It's like "iron sharpening iron"—glory motivates soldiers
and makes them braver. In high-stakes enterprises, "glorious" personalities put energy into the business, while
"sober" people are just "ballast" (they keep things steady but don't move the ship).

In academia, a bit of "ostentation" helps your work survive. Socrates and Aristotle weren't just smart; they knew
how to present themselves. Vanity is like a "varnish"—it makes the work shine and last longer.

But don't confuse this with "natural magnanimity." Some people are just naturally great and discreet, and their
modesty is actually a "pro move" to get more things done. Being liberal with praise for others is actually a "flex"
of your own perfection—because if the person you're praising is better than you, you're acknowledging excellence;
if they're worse, you're showing your own generosity.

In the end, "glorious" people are just a joke to the wise, idols to the suck-ups, and slaves to their own hype.
