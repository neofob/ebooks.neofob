# OF TRAVEL (THE ULTIMATE GLOW-UP)

For young people, traveling is basically a school; for older people, it's a way to gain experience. If you go to
a country without knowing the language first, you're not "traveling"—you're just taking a class. I'm totally
down with young people traveling with a tutor or a serious assistant, as long as that person knows the language
and has been there before. They can tell the kids what's actually worth seeing, who they should network with,
and what skills they can pick up. Otherwise, young people just wander around "hooded" and miss everything.

It's wild that people keep diaries on sea voyages where there's literally nothing to see but sky and water, but
when they travel on land—where there's actually stuff to observe—they skip the diary. Like, why? Let's make
diaries a thing again.

The "must-see" list:
- The courts of princes (especially when they're meeting ambassadors).
- The courts of justice (while they're actually handling cases).
- Church councils, monasteries, and the monuments inside them.
- City walls and fortifications.
- Harbors, ruins, libraries, and colleges.
- Academic debates and lectures.
- Shipping and navies.
- Fancy houses and gardens near big cities.
- Armories, arsenals, warehouses, and stock exchanges.
- Horse riding, fencing, and military training.
- High-end comedies and plays where the "based" people hang out.
- Jewel collections and rare artifacts.
Basically, any "memorable" thing in the city—the tutors should be doing the research on this. Triumphs, masks,
feasts, weddings, funerals, and executions are also worth seeing, though they're usually obvious.

If you want a young person to maximize their trip in a short time:
1. Learn the language *before* you go.
2. Have a tutor who knows the country.
3. Carry a map or a guidebook to use as a key for your research.
4. Keep a diary.
5. Don't stay in one city too long—just enough to get the vibe. When you're in a city, change your hotels/lodgings
from one side of town to the other; this is a "cheat code" for meeting new people.
6. Stop hanging out with people from your own country. Eat at places where the locals hang out.
7. Get a recommendation to someone high-status in the next city before you move, so you can use their clout to
see things you otherwise wouldn't.

For networking: the most "high-value" contacts are the secretaries and assistants of ambassadors. By talking to
one, you basically get the experience of many different countries. Also, visit famous people to see if their real
life matches their hype.

Avoid drama. Most travel quarrels are about mistresses, drinking, status, or just words. Avoid "angry" or "toxic"
people, because they'll drag you into their own beef.

When you get home, don't just forget everything. Keep writing to the high-value people you met. Let your travel
show in your *conversations*, not in your clothes or the way you act. And when you talk, be a listener rather than
just a storyteller. Don't replace your own culture's manners with foreign ones; just "prick in" a few beautiful
flowers from abroad into your own customs.
