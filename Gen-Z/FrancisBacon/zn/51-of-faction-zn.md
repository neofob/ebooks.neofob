# OF FACTION (SQUAD WARS)

Some people think it's a "big brain" move for a leader to govern based on factions, but that's actually mid. The real
wisdom is either focusing on the "universal" goals that everyone agrees on, or dealing with people one-on-one. But
let's be real: you can't just ignore factions entirely.

If you're a "nobody" trying to rise, you have to pick a side. But if you're already a "somebody" with your own
power, it's better to stay neutral and not get locked into a squad. Even for beginners, the best move is to join
the faction that's the most "chill" and likely to be accepted by the other side.

Interesting fact: the smaller, "stiffer" faction is often the one that holds things together, while the big,
moderate squad just gets tired and gives up. When one faction gets deleted, the remaining one usually starts
fighting with itself—like the Roman senators after the authority was pulled down. This also happens in private
drama. Sometimes the "sidekicks" in a faction become the main characters when the group splits, but other times
they just become "ciphers" and get ghosted because their only value was opposing someone else.

It's common for people to switch sides once they've reached the top, thinking they've secured the first bag and
are ready for a new one. The "traitor" in a faction often gets away with it because when things are balanced for
a long time, one person switching sides can tip the whole scale—and then they get all the credit.

The only way to actually stay neutral is to be true to yourself and use both sides for your own ends. In Italy,
they find it "sus" when a Pope calls himself "the common father"—they think he's just trying to make his own
family more powerful.

Kings need to be careful not to become "part of a faction." Making "leagues" inside the state is toxic for a
monarchy because it creates a loyalty that's higher than the loyalty to the king. It basically turns the king
into "just one of the boys." When factions get too violent, it's a sign the prince is weak, and it ruins both the
authority and the business of the state.

Faction movements should be like the orbits of planets: they can have their own little spins, but they're still
quietly carried by the "prime mover" (the king).
